## Declarative Manifests
Each content pack exports a `GameContentManifest` (`src/engine/types/manifest.ts`). The `GameEngine` constructor registers its entries, for example: `monsters`, `spells`, `traps`, `statusEffects`/`statusHandlers`, `aiBehaviors`/`aiStrategies`, `actionHooks`, `affinityMatrix`, `pacts`, `progressionConfig`, `initialWorldState`, `quest`, and `theme`. Content assembles behavior from composable primitives: status afflictions, spawners, stat triggers, damage affinities, drop tables, and quest arcs. A status handler marked `affliction` (the built-in poison, paralysis, slow, blindness and stun; cotw's burning) is one a cure of every affliction, a potion's `cure_status: 'all'`, purges; an unmarked status (haste, a channel, a floor's darkness) stays. Presentation data rides along: `branding`/`town.name` supply shared screens' pack wording (including `branding.xpName`, the experience term the HUD, log and memorial use, "XP" when absent; and `branding.manaName`/`manaUnit`, the spell resource's name and the unit amounts are written in, resolved by `resolveManaTerms`, "Mana"/"MP" when absent), `firstTimeHints` gives a short note per system the HUD introduces once per hero (`FirstTimeHintId`: altar, killRite, pactKeeper, companion, renown, runeOfReturn, factionStanding, story; `{key:<action>}` in its text names the bound key; a system without one shows no hint), and a `spriteRecipes` key equal to a definition ID gives that entity its own atlas cell (`SpriteAtlas`). The renderer's built-in cells and tag rules (`DEFAULT_TAG_SPRITE_ORDER`) cover only generic archetypes; a pack's own creature tags map to its recipes through `atlas.spriteTagRules`, checked first, and its zone and town-exterior terrain (`<base>_<zoneKey>`, `floor_town_outdoor`) are its own recipes too. A pack that declares `atlas.terrain` gets neighbour-aware terrain from `src/rendering/atlas/terrain-layers.ts`: floors pick among tone variants by smooth world-space noise (optionally a 2x2 macro tile), walls take a face when open to the south and rim or corner overlays on other open sides, liquids and pits blend as one area with edges toward other tiles, and pillars, bars, stairs, traps, doors and the pack's own tiles draw as props over the floor. Doors between rock east and west sit in the wall line; doors never cast a contact shadow, so a corridor joins its room without a break. Terrain cells bake flat (no sprite outline or diagonal shading), `terrain.memory` replaces the blue-slate dimming of remembered cells, and `terrain.torch`/`emissive` light visible cells (the player's Torchlight setting turns it off; a lit town, `town.lit`, keeps only the emissive glow). Without `atlas.terrain`, a pack keeps one recipe per tile type (`getTerrainSpriteKey`), which paints water, chasms, bars and pillars as floor; `drawTacticalTerrain` marks those over it in the theme's role colors (it no longer picks looks by floor number), as it does any such cell the pack's terrain recipes miss. Surfaces and gases are the pack's art too: `atlas.overlays` maps `surface~<type>` and `gas~<type>` to a `CellOverlayArt`, which, unlike a recipe, is not baked into the atlas but runs every frame over the terrain, in canvas units at the cell's place, given the cell and the frame's clock so fire can flicker without content reading the clock (cotw's are in `terrain/overlays.ts`). `IntentOverlay.renderSurfaces` draws a type with no art as a neutral wash (the theme's `warn` for a surface, `textSoft` for a gas), so a surface or gas the pack draws no art for never acts unseen.

### Item Families
Equipment found as loot may belong to one of seven families — Blessed, Enchanted, Holy (positive), Cursed, Hexed, Unholy (negative), Chaotic — in addition to its separate +N roll ([ADR-0012](../decisions/0012-item-families-as-pack-data.md)). The engine owns the family ids, the three alignments and what each modifier field does; `manifest.itemFamilies` (`ItemFamilyConfig`) supplies the pack's tiers (names, numbers, the floor each starts on), each family's expected count per game, its first floor and whether it binds, and `itemsPerGame`, the measured number of eligible items a game offers. `rollItemFamily` (`src/engine/items/modifierRoller.ts`) rolls Normal first, then a family by `perGame / itemsPerGame`, then the deepest tier the floor has reached; a family below its `minFloor` gives its share to Normal. Only loot rolls: `createScaledItem` applies the config when a caller passes it (floor loot, chests, vault chests, the boss hoard, monster drops), and shop stock, starting gear, choice rewards and kill-rite essences stay Normal. `ItemDefinition.family` pins a definition to one family, its tier by floor. A pack without `itemFamilies` gets only Normal items and an unchanged loot stream. What a modifier does is its fields, read in one place each (`ItemModifier`): melee and spell scaling, mana discount, damage taken, `tagBonuses` against a defender tag (`living` is "not undead"; a bonus may heal the attacker a share, `healPercentOfDamage`), `binds`, `sacredGroundBurn` (the bearer burns stepping onto or striking from sacred ground — a `TileDefinition.sacred` altar or a `TownBuildingDefinition.sacred` temple, `isSacredGround`) and `templeShunned` (the temple serves only the cleanse, at double the price; `TownServicesDefinition.templeShunnedMessage`); and the rule-benders the Chaotic family is made of — `randomSpellElement`, `killHealPercent`, `forbidsRest`, `extraMeleeStrikes`/`missSelfDamage`, `evasionBonus`, `blinkEverySteps`/`blinkRange`, `meleeDamageRoll`, `overflowNoDebt`/`overflowTierShift`/`overflowTierShiftCap`, `reflectMeleePercent`, `healingReceivedMultiplier`; and the perk effects added by tracker 3.6 (any item may carry them too): `belowHalfHpMeleeMultiplier`, `manaCostMultiplier`, `perceptionRadiusMultiplier`, `meleeDamageTakenMultiplier`, `impulseImmune`, `killManaPercent`, `overflowDebtDecayMultiplier`, `carryMultiplier` (`Player.carryStrength`), `knockbackBonus`, `onHitStatus`, `rangedHitBonus`, `rangedDamageBonus` (no cotw perk uses them: nothing fires a ranged attack in play), `meleeHitBonus`, `shortenedAfflictions` (`afflictionDuration`, read by melee bites and hook procs), `identifiesCarriedOnStairs` (`identifyCarriedSinceStairs`, on the stairs), `sightBonus` (`Player.sightBonus`, added to the FOV radius in `GameEngine.updateFov`; [ADR-0013](../decisions/0013-sight-bonus-in-fov-radius.md)), `followUpStrikeShare` (a half-damage second strike), `elementSpellMultiplier` (spells of one element), `resistsElements` (`Actor.affinityTo`: neutral becomes resisted, a weakness neutral; every element-affinity read goes through `affinityTo`), `grimoireSynergyRepeats` (each neighbor synergy counts again), `critChanceBonus`/`critMultiplier` (melee criticals in a pack with no base crit), `companionStatMultiplier` (`bondCompanion`, once per companion on its turn) and `companionRisesPerFloor`, `lastStandPerFloor` (both in `refusesDeath`, which `DeathResolver.resolveDeath` asks first; "once per floor" is once per visit, `floorVisitKey`), `sensesAllMonsters` (`sensesThroughWalls`, which the renderer asks beside Detect Monsters), `spellRangeBonus` (`withSpellRangeBonus`, at cast and in `resolveCast`), `defensePenetration` (beside Anatomist's half), `shieldWithTwoHanded` (`Paperdoll.shieldBesideTwoHanded`, installed by `Player`; `Paperdoll.displacedBy` is the one place a two-hander's blocking is decided), `ripostesOnEvade` and `evadeBlinkRange` (`MeleeAttackAction.afterEvade`), `evasionPerIntelligence` (`intelligenceEvasion`), `trapImmune` (`TrapInstance.trigger`, and an impulse onto a trap tile), `restHealMultiplier` (`AutoRestManager.recoverRestTurn`, both rest paths), `grantsStatusImmunities` (added to `statusImmunities` when a perk is granted; worn, it holds only while worn: `Actor.isImmuneTo`, which `StatusManager.applyStatus` asks beside the caller's list), `chainExtraHops`, `firstSpellPerFloorMultiplier` (`wardFirstSpell`), `maxHpPercent` (`Player.wornMaxHpPercent` in `calculateAttribute`; a choice granting it raises HP with the max), `levelUpFullHeal`, and a negative `overflowTierShift` (a milder surge; below the first tier none); and those a family perk scopes to one monster family (`familyModifiers`): `meleeKnockback`, `sensesWithin`, `coinMultiplier`, `xpMultiplier`, `afflictionShrugChance` (`shrugsAffliction`, asked on every path a monster afflicts by: a melee bite, a spell's status, a hook), `windUpWarningBonus`, `windUpDamageTakenMultiplier` (a breath or slam), `wakeRadius` (also worn or held: `wakesOnSight`, [ADR-0014](../decisions/0014-wake-radius-in-fov-awakening.md)), besides `damageTakenMultiplier` and `defensePenetration` from the family. Tiers that share the deepest floor reached are variants, one picked at random (cotw's eight Chaotic effects all start on floor 1). Damage taken (`damageTakenMultiplier`) and healing received scale in `Actor.takeDamage`/`Actor.heal`, so they apply whatever dealt the blow or the cure. A definition can give its item any of these fields outright, family or not: `ItemDefinition.wornEffects` (a `PerkEffects`), which `wornModifiers` adds while the item is worn. cotw's Ring of the Slag-Walker, Zealot's Seared Crown and the Obsidian Scale Cuirass resist fire, the Soot-Visored Helm keeps blindness off, the Rootless Striders paralysis and traps, the Corpse-Chieftain's Eye-Coin stuns; the Pit-Draugr Pick and the Quicksilver Stiletto pierce armour (`defensePenetration`), the Ironwood Bough-Stave raises spell damage, the Amber Heart-Drop max HP and Thrym's girdle the carrying Strength (owner, 2026-10-07). `itemDescriptions.test.ts` fails a description that promises a resistance or immunity without one.

## Two Hook Mechanisms
1. **Action hooks** (`manifest.actionHooks`, interface `ActionHook` in `actionPipeline.ts`): priority-ordered `pre`/`post` hooks around pipeline-executed actions. They receive `ActionHookContext` (`action`, `engine`, `actionType`, and `result` in the post phase). A pre-hook can short-circuit with its own `ActionResult`. Hooks fire for every actor; `ActionHookContext.actor` identifies who is acting, so content that should react only to the player compares it against `engine.player`.
2. **Declarative event hooks** (`HookDispatcher` in `src/engine/hooks/hookDispatcher.ts`): `HookDescriptor` data (event, chance, predicate, primitive action) attached to items and monsters. They receive `HookContext` (`engine`, `attacker`, `defender`, `damage`, …). The action is one of the engine's built-in primitives (`ActionPrimitive`: `applyStatus`, `spawnSurface`, `spawnGas`, `heal`, `bonusDamage`); content cannot register another, and there are no global hooks. Every way a monster is made from its definition (floor, vault, wandering and boss spawns, and a load) carries the definition's `hooks`. `onTurnStart` fires at the start of an awake monster's turn, after its status tick, unless it is stunned or paralyzed; its `defender` is the entity the monster engages when that one is within 8 tiles and in line of sight. A primitive aimed at `target: 'target'` with no one there is skipped rather than landing on the hook's owner. `onHit` (the attacker's hooks) and `onDamageTaken` (the defender's, when wounded) fire for every blow with an attacker: melee, spell damage, chain-lightning hops and monster wind-ups (`dispatchDamageHooks`, `src/engine/hooks/damageHooks.ts`), before the blow's death resolves; not for a blow the defender was immune to or absorbed. `onBlock` is melee's alone.

## Injected Context
Handlers get the live engine through an injected context object rather than importing engine runtime state. Both context types expose `EngineContext` (`src/engine/types/engineContext.ts`), not the raw `GameEngine`: `player`, `map`, `surfaces`, `worldState`, the seeded `rng`, `log()`, the world-flag accessors, `currentFloor`, `removeEntity()` (those two added so a pack's hooks can scope an encounter to its floor and take its NPCs off the map through the scheduler-aware path, not `map.removeEntity`), `addEntity()` (added for tracker 5.3 so a pack can bring a monster onto the floor mid-visit, into the scheduler too: cotw's dark floors draw monsters out of the dark), and `compendium` narrowed to `getEntry` (added so a pack can answer a kind of death, however it happened: an action's own result does not yet carry its events when post-hooks run). `GameEngine` satisfies it structurally, so nothing is wrapped at runtime and the narrowing is enforced by the type checker — a handler reaching for `engine.scheduler` no longer compiles. Widen it deliberately: each member added becomes part of the content-facing contract. The dispatcher's own built-in primitives (damage, death, statuses, surfaces) are engine code rather than handlers and narrow back through a single documented cast.

## Content Registries
Manifest registration writes into per-engine registries owned by `GameEngine` via `engine.registries` (`EngineRegistries`). Content registries (monsters, traps, spells, companions, AI strategies, AI behaviors, status handlers, tiles) are seeded from process defaults upon engine creation and supplemented by manifest registration, so two engines built from different manifests do not collide. Runtime state registries (`containers`, `itemIndex`) are per-engine and isolated, populated dynamically from the live world state rather than seeded from process defaults. Module-level registries (e.g. `MonsterRegistry`, `SpellRegistry`, `CompanionRegistry`, `StatusHandlers`, `TileRegistry`, `containerRegistry`, `itemIndex`) act as facades that forward into whichever engine's store is active (`activateRegistries(engine.registries)`), holding no state maps of their own, so each engine maintains independent lookups. Traps have no facade: an engine's trap definitions are `engine.registries.traps`, and the trap generator places traps from `manifest.traps`.
