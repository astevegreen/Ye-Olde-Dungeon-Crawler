# Companions & Pet Progression

> Topic doc under [content-extensibility.md](content-extensibility.md) ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) §3). Explanatory only — every binding rule about content integration lives on the guaranteed-read path (`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s §3 stub and `content-extensibility.md`'s core sections), never only here. Flag a conflict rather than resolving it here (§8.2).

`Companion extends Monster` (`src/engine/entities/companion.ts`), with `faction: 'player'` so the existing generic `isHostileTo()` already treats it as an ally and hostile monsters as hostile to it — no changes to the hostility system. `manifest.companions` (`CompanionDefinition[]`) declares content-defined companions (stats, speed, pack-mule capacity, growth per hero level), registered via `CompanionRegistry`. `GameEngine.summonCompanion()`/`dismissCompanion()` manage the single active companion (at most one at a time); it travels with the player across `changeFloor()` transitions, and persists as a top-level `SaveData.companion` field (schema v9) rather than as part of any one floor's monster list.

## Growth With the Hero's Level
A definition may declare `growthPerLevel` (`{ hp?, attack?, defense? }`, fractions allowed): the companion's max HP, attack and defense are then its `stats` plus that growth for each hero level past the first, rounded, with max HP and attack times Beast-Friend's `companionStatMultiplier` once that bond has taken. `growCompanion` (`combat/lastStand.ts`, beside `bondCompanion`) recomputes them from the definition when the hero levels (`awardPlayerXp`, F2's level grant), when one is summoned, granted or revived, after a load (so a save from before growth loads grown), and on the companion's own turn, which catches anything else. The HP a change adds is added to what it has, so a wound stays a wound. No new save field: the stats are saved as before and recomputed from the definition. A companion without `growthPerLevel` keeps its `stats` (R-cotw-18 found cotw's 22–30 HP companions a one-hit kill on floor 25). `cotw` (`companions.ts`, owner 2026-10-07) gives about the hero's +5 HP a level: the battle-hound +5 HP, +0.6 attack, +0.3 defense; the Frost-Ward Hound +5, +0.4, +0.5; the Ember-Fang Wolf +4, +0.8, +0.25 (at hero level 25: 150/20/9, 146/14/17, 118/28/8).

## Acquisition Gate
`summonCompanion()` refuses until `GameEngine.COMPANION_BONDED_FLAG` (`WorldState` flag) is set. `TrainerService.bondCompanion()` (a trainer-NPC-role `NpcRole`, e.g. town's "Ranvild the Hound-Warden") sets it for a one-time gold cost — a companion is quest/purchase-gated, not available from turn one.

## AI-Targeting Generalization
`selectAttackTarget(engine, actor)` (`ai/targetSelection.ts`) resolves a monster's attack/pathing target, defaulting to `engine.player` — behaviorally identical to the old hardcoded behavior — unless `Monster.targetingMode === 'nearest_hostile'` (`MonsterDefinition.targetingMode`, opt-in per monster, bounded-radius nearest-hostile search). No shipped monster's difficulty changes unless its content definition opts in; in `cotw`, `wolf`, `garmling`, and `brim_howler` do today. This is the prerequisite that lets a companion actually draw aggro and "tank."

## Passing in a Corridor
Walking into the companion trades places with it (`actions/movement.ts`, the same rule that lets the hero past a neutral monster), so a companion in a one-tile corridor never walls the hero in. Its follow AI takes it from there.

## Archetypes
`Companion.archetype` (`'balanced' | 'bodyguard' | 'skirmisher'`) selects one of three `AIStrategy` implementations (`ai/aiRegistry.ts`): `companion_follow` (balanced, follow distance 2 — Phase 1's original behavior), `companion_bodyguard` (follow distance 1, stays tight to the player), `companion_skirmisher` (follow distance 5, proactively paths to a hostile within a 6-tile seek radius even before it's adjacent). `Companion.setArchetype()` switches both the archetype and its backing `aiRoutineId` together; `TrainerService.switchArchetype()` is the paid, trainer-gated entry point.

## Death & Revival
A dying companion is intercepted by `DeathResolver` (duck-typed via `companionDefinitionId`, not an `instanceof Companion` check, to avoid a value import into the `entities/monster.ts` circular-import cycle) before the generic `Monster` death pipeline (no XP or loot) and kept as `engine.deadCompanionRecord` (saved as `SaveData.deadCompanion`) rather than discarded; while it is set, `summonCompanion()` refuses and points the hero to a trainer, so a fallen companion can't be replaced for free. A dismissed companion is kept too (`engine.dismissedCompanion`, saved as `SaveData.dismissedCompanion`), and summoning it back returns the same instance, wounds and pack intact; the summon command calls the hero's own companion (dismissed or fallen) before the pack's first. A companion's trainer-taught `archetype` and `unlockedSkills` are saved with it. `TrainerService.reviveCompanion()` heals and reattaches the same instance — pack contents intact — for a gold cost. A living companion is explicitly excluded from the floor-monster-count that triggers floor-clear, since it is player-aligned, not a hostile the floor needs cleared of.

## Active Skills
`Companion.unlockedSkills: string[]`, taught via `TrainerService.teachSkill()` (paid, trainer-gated) and invoked via the `use_companion_skill` command. The trainer's dialog offers the skills the pack lists in `TownServicesDefinition.trainerSkills` (id, name, description), one key each; a pack that lists none has no teaching offer. Each listed skill carries its `effect` (`CompanionSkillEffect`: a share of the companion's max HP healed, a status for the hero, the line logged), which `use_companion_skill` applies; the engine names no skill, and `check:engine-creep` collects the listed ids. cotw's trainer teaches Rally Howl (a fifth of the companion's HP, three turns of haste for the hero). The palette's Companion Skill command, like the key, uses the companion's first learned skill. Two bindable keys reach the companion: `companion_call` (Shift+C) summons it or, when it is at hand, dismisses it, and `companion_skill` (Shift+R) has it use its first learned skill; the input handler checks them before the bare C and R hotkeys, and the F1 exploration card lists them for a pack with companions.

## Inventory Transfer
Items move both ways between the player and the companion's own `InventoryManager`/pack. In the Inventory tab (`src/ui/inventory/inventoryController.ts`), `[G]` or a drop on the companion's column gives the selected backpack item (`transfer_to_companion`); `[K]` switches the third column to the companion's pack, where `[T]`, `[Enter]`, a double-click or a drag to the backpack takes an item back (`transfer_from_companion`), and `[Esc]` returns the column to the ground without closing the menu. `EngineCommandBus.resolveItem` treats the active companion's pack as reachable, alongside carried, worn, and underfoot items — taking an item back is exactly that case.
