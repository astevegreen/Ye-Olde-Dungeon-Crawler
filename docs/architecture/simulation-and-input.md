# Simulation Scoping & Input Architecture

> Sub-document of [`ARCHITECTURE.md`](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) §6 stub. This file is **explanatory**: mechanism detail and worked examples. Every **binding** rule — bounded simulation scoping, the modal-stack focus rule — is restated in the core document's §6 stub. If this file and the core document ever disagree, the core document wins; flag the conflict rather than resolving it here (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §8.2).

- **Bounded Simulation Scoping:** Simulating multiple floors at once is prohibited. Visited floors other than the active one are stored (`engine.storedFloors`) and not simulated. Within the active floor, per-actor work is throttled:
  - **Dormant Actor Scheduling:** Every living actor on the active floor stays in `EnergyScheduler` and accrues energy. On a monster's turn, `Monster.takeTurn()` does the following, in order:
    1. Resolves status-effect ticks (`statusManager.tick()`), always, sleeping or not.
    2. Checks paralysis/stun.
    - Then, for an awake monster with `onTurnStart` hooks, dispatches them (`HookDispatcher`); a sleeper skips this.
    3. Calls `MonsterAI.decideAction()`. Inside it, any pending wind-up and the spell-cooldown decrement resolve first. A monster with `aiState === 'sleeping'` then wakes if it has line of sight to the player within Euclidean distance 8 or stands on a tile visible in the player's FOV, unless it is farther off than the hero's wake radius (`wakesOnSight`: a worn or perk `wakeRadius`, or the family perk's against its family; [ADR-0014](../decisions/0014-wake-radius-in-fov-awakening.md)). Otherwise it returns a `WaitAction` without pathfinding, item use, or combat resolution.
    - `GameEngine.updateFov()` also wakes sleeping monsters on visible tiles, with the same wake-radius check (`Monster.wakesOnSight`); damage and alarms wake a sleeper regardless.
    - Companions (§3) never sleep (`aiState: 'hunting'` always) and are registered on `EnergyScheduler` like any other active-floor actor — no new scoping exception to this bounded-simulation model.
  - **Memory:** a hostile monster knows where its target is only while it perceives it (`perceives` in `src/engine/ai/perception.ts`: beside it, or in line of sight within its sight radius, 8, cut to the `perceptionRadius` of any status it suffers as the hero's is, so a blinded monster perceives only what is beside it; and never a target standing in an opaque gas, dense steam or smoke, beyond arm's reach). Each such turn it stores the tile in `Monster.pursuit` (saved); out of its senses it walks there, searches around it (3 tiles) for 10 turns, then gives up and stands until it sees the target again (`src/engine/ai/pursuit.ts`; the Look card reads "Searching for you"). A monster with no memory yet knows where the target is: just woken, struck (`Monster.takeDamage`), or alerted (`Monster.alert`: the alarm trap, a choice's `alertMonsters`), so an alarm brings the floor to where it rang. A turret (`holdsGround`) keeps its post. A companion always knows its hero.
  - **Pursuit:** an awake monster closing on a goal, its target or the tile it remembers (`stepTowardAction` in `src/engine/ai/pursuit.ts`), runs its own path search only within 10 tiles, and that search gives up after the 21×21 tiles around it, so a monster walled in by a crowd stays cheap. Beyond that, or when its own search fails, it steps along the floor's shared distance field to that goal (`src/engine/ai/distanceField.ts`): one breadth-first pass per goal per turn (up to 8 goals kept), cached per map, serves every monster headed there, so its own cost is a look at its eight neighbours. Before this, an awake monster more than 10 tiles away stood still for good.
  - **Doors:** a monster's path search and the distance field open closed doors and treat a locked one as a wall. A fleeing monster (`fleeAction` in `src/engine/ai/flight.ts`) opens a closed door in its way and shuts an open one it has put between itself and the hero, unless the hero stands beside that door.
  - **Breaking and running:** a monster that drops below its flee line calls for help once (`callForHelp`, `src/engine/ai/flight.ts`): every ally of its faction within 8 that isn't running is alerted (`Monster.alert`); the log says so when the hero sees the caller or one answers. While it runs it heads for the nearest ally within 10 that stands nearer it than the hero, never through a step beside the hero, and stands with it once there; with none, it runs straight away. Both are bounded box scans (§6).
  - **Casters behind the line:** a kiting caster with an awake melee ally of its faction within 6 that stands at least as near the hero (`hasMeleeScreen`, `src/engine/ai/aiRegistry.ts`) stays behind it: between casts it holds at range, steps back when within 2, and steps away rather than trade blows when the hero reaches it. Alone, it still closes in to fight between casts.
  - **Packs:** a monster definition's `pack` (`{ size: [min, max] }`) makes it hunt in packs. Drawn for a room's population (`populateDungeonFloor`), it brings packmates of its kind and the pack is that room's population; with the melee routine each member within 10 of its prey closes on a free side of it (`flankAction`, `src/engine/ai/packTactics.ts`): one breadth-first search over the 21×21 square around it, a side scored by its steps plus 4 for each packmate already beside it, never passing beside the prey on the way.
  - **Scheduler Partitioning (Evaluated, Not Adopted):** an active/dormant scheduler partition was implemented, benchmarked, and reverted. See [ADR-0001](../decisions/0001-scheduler-partitioning-evaluated-not-adopted.md) for the full evidence and rationale. `EnergyScheduler` uses a single flat entity list today. Review that ADR before re-attempting the same partition.
  - **Bounded FOV Awakenings:** `FovManager` computes visibility with recursive shadowcasting limited to the player's radius (default 8, adjusted by pact modifiers). Monster awakenings and bestiary records are checked only across the bounding box `[minX..maxX, minY..maxY]` of the player's vision. Each update demotes only the tiles the previous pass lit: `FovManager` tracks them as packed indices, so the cost follows the view, not the map. Anything marked visible outside the shadowcasting pass (`setVisibility`) is tracked the same way, and `revealAll` sets a flag that makes the next update fall back to one full sweep — tracking every index there would cost more than the sweep it saves. `lastDemotedTiles` exposes the count for diagnostics. On a lit map (`GameMap.lit`: the town when `TownLayoutDefinition.lit` is set) the shadowcasting reaches the whole map, so the hero sees every tile in line of sight; a radius under 2 is a senses limit (blindness, sensory masking) and is kept, while the hero's own radius is never under 2. The awakening and bestiary box stays the hero's radius. `lit` is not saved: `TownMapGenerator` sets it from the manifest, and `restoreFloorLight` re-derives it for floor 0 on a load and when the floor archive rehydrates the town.
  - **Perception-Radius Override:** any active status whose handler declares `StatusHandler.perceptionRadius` forces `updateFov()`'s radius to that value (the most restrictive wins if several are active) — a generic mechanism, not a hardcoded per-status check. `blindness` and `sensory_masked` both declare `perceptionRadius: 1` today. A pack may declare its own: cotw's dark floors (`content/cotw/darkness.ts`, tracker 5.3; floor 21, relit by shattering the Siphon Pylon's core, `siphonPylon.ts`; floor 30, relit when Gloom-Tarr dies, `bileSump.ts`; a dark floor made before it went dark, with nothing there to relight it, keeps its light) give the hero `cotw:darkness` (2) or, with a torch (an item of category `light`) in the off hand, `cotw:torchlit` (5), kept in step by a post action hook while the floor is dark; each status removes itself in `onTick` once the floor is left or relit. The same statuses limit a monster's sight (`ai/perception.ts`), but the dark floors put none on monsters: they see in the dark, and are `cotw:emboldened` there (a quarter more attack).
  - **Sensory Masking & Echolocation:** the `sensory_masked` status (content-applied, e.g. a Bat Senses Potion) cripples FOV to radius 1 via the override above, and separately lets the renderer detect things beyond it: `getAudibleEntitiesInRadius`/`getAudibleTilesInRadius` (`src/engine/fov/echolocation.ts`, radius `ECHOLOCATION_HEARING_RADIUS` = 6) find non-dormant actors (a sleeping monster makes no noise) and noisy terrain (inherently audible tile types, or an active surface/gas/trap) within a bounded query. `CanvasRenderer.renderEcholocationView()` (`src/rendering/canvas-renderer.ts`) replaces the normal tile/entity pass while the status is active: a blank board, the player's own icon, and only audible actors (reusing the existing ESP-detected pulsing-indicator style) and terrain. The underlying FOV/awakening simulation is unchanged — only what gets drawn changes.
  - **Spatial Bucketing:** Entity lookups use constant-time coordinate bucket maps (`entityBuckets: Map<string, Entity[]>` keyed `"x,y"` in `GameMap`), so proximity and collision checks do not scale with total monster count.
  - **Modal Pause:** Pushing the first modal onto `ModalStackManager` sets `engine.isPaused`, and `advanceWorldUntilPlayerTurn()` does nothing while paused. The pause stops idle advancement only: a player action a menu issues through the command bus (a potion drunk, armour swapped, the menu left open) lifts it for that action, so the monsters answer it as they would with the menu closed.
- **Input Architecture:** `InputHandler` (`src/rendering/input-handler.ts`) owns the `window` `keydown`/`keyup`/`blur` listeners, the `ModalStackManager`, and the `ChordBuffer`.
  - **Modifier chords:** a key held with Ctrl, Alt or Meta drives nothing in the game and is left to the browser (Ctrl+F finds, Ctrl+R reloads), except `Ctrl`/`Cmd+K`, the command palette, and Alt with a movement key (arrows, numpad, Home/End/PageUp/PageDown), which does nothing at all: Alt+Left/Right/Home is the browser's Back, Forward and Home page, which would leave the game.
- **`ChordBuffer` Mechanics (`src/ui/input/chordBuffer.ts`):**
  - **Sliding-Window Arrow Chording:** When `arrowChordingEnabled` is on (the default), orthogonal arrow keypresses within the micro-debounce window (`arrowChordBufferMs`, default 40ms, clamped to 25–75ms) combine into a diagonal (e.g., Up + Right -> NorthEast). When it is off, arrows move cardinally with no buffering.
  - **Keyup Flush:** Releasing an arrow key before the debounce timer expires dispatches the pending cardinal step immediately — `handleKeyUp` calls `flush()` when the released key is the one that started the pending step, since no chord can follow it. Waiting out the window after the key is already up is latency the player feels on every step. A chord that formed before release still wins, and the release emits nothing further.
  - **Key-Repeat Bypass:** While an arrow key or an active diagonal chord is held and the browser sends repeat events (`KeyboardEvent.repeat`), the buffer skips the debounce timer and dispatches a move on each repeat.
  - **Opposing Direction Reversal:** Pressing the opposite key while a step is pending (e.g. Left while Right is pending) cancels the pending move and immediately honors the new direction.
  - **Focus Loss:** Window `blur` clears all held-key state.
  - **Multi-Scheme Directional Controls:** Default bindings live in `src/ui/settings/settingsManager.ts`. Only arrow keys go through `ChordBuffer`; the other schemes dispatch immediately:
    1. Arrow keys (chorded or instant cardinal mode).
    2. Roguelike Numpad 1–9: diagonals 7, 9, 1, 3; cardinals 8, 2, 4, 6; center 5 to wait.
    3. Vi keys: H, J, K, Y, N by default. L, U, and B open Look, the Character tab, and the Bestiary, and keep those keys (ADR-0011).
    4. WASD: W, A, D by default; S is Search.
    - Every default key maps to exactly one action; `src/ui/__tests__/defaultKeybinds.test.ts` enforces it, including the keys `InputHandler` hard-wires. Those keys, which it answers before reading any binding, are listed in `HARD_WIRED_KEYS` (`settingsManager.ts`): Settings refuses to bind one to any other action, movement included, and says what the key already does. A key pressed with Shift is recorded as the chord `Shift+<code>`, the form `InputHandler` reads before the bare key (movement included), and is refused as its bare key would be unless it is one of the action's own default chords; a lone modifier is never recorded, and Ctrl, Alt and Cmd chords are refused, since they belong to the browser (Modifier chords, above). Key chips show human names (`src/ui/keyLabel.ts`).
- **Configurable Radial Action Menu (`src/rendering/radialMenu.ts`):** a hold-to-open canvas overlay bound to the configurable `radial_menu` action (default `KeyV`, remappable through the same `SettingsManager`/`ACTION_METADATA` keybind system as every other action). While held, the same directional-key vocabulary above (arrows/WASD/vi/numpad) selects one of 8 compass wedges instead of moving; releasing the trigger key confirms the hovered wedge, `Escape` cancels. Slots (`SettingsManager.radialMenuSlots`, keyed by the fixed 8-direction order) are `{ type: 'spell' }`, `{ type: 'command' }` (dispatched via `CommandPalette.getCommand`), or `{ type: 'item' }` (a potion or self-targeted scroll resolved via `InventoryManager.findItemById` — aimed items that need a target reticle, like wands, are not a fit for direct radial activation). It registers on `ModalStackManager` like other overlays. While every slot is empty the key opens nothing and logs "The radial menu is empty."; a window blur closes and repaints it. Gamepad invocation is not implemented and is deliberately out of scope. **[Deferred: P-24]**
- **Potion row (`src/ui/potionRow.ts`):** four slots beside the health orb, each pinned to a potion kind (`Player.quickPotions`, saved with the character; keyed by `potionKindKey`: definition ID, else the true name, never shown). The first time a hero has no pins, the kinds they carry are pinned in order; after that only the player changes pins, by right-clicking a slot or clicking an empty one (a picker that registers on `ModalStackManager`). A slot whose kind has run out stays, dimmed, with a zero count. A click or the `drink_potion_1..4` actions (default `Shift+Digit1..4`) drink via `handlePlayerAction(DrinkPotionAction)`, the radial menu's path; a key on an empty slot opens its picker. `InputHandler` checks these bindings before its bare-digit quick-spell keys, which would otherwise read `Shift+1` as spell slot 1.
- **Command palette (`src/ui/help/commandPalette.ts`):** a `UIModal` that pushes itself onto `ModalStackManager` when it opens and removes itself when it closes. `Ctrl+K`/`Cmd+K` or `Shift+/` toggles it (`InputHandler.onToggleCommandPalette`), only when no other modal is on top.
- **Focus & Modal Isolation:**
  - **Rule:** every open modal registers on the LIFO `ModalStackManager` (`src/ui/modalStack.ts`).
  - **Stack behavior:** the top modal receives all keystrokes. If it does not handle `Escape`, the stack pops it. All other keys are trapped so they never reach the simulation. Modal handlers call `event.preventDefault()` for keys they consume, which prevents browser shortcut conflicts.
  - **Keyboard focus:** keys reach `InputHandler`'s `window` listener whatever has focus, but focus left on a HUD button behind a dialog let Tab walk out onto the HUD. The soak's chaos runs logged it 963 times in one night, behind the potion picker, the character menu, the shop, the choice and the diagnostics.
    - A modal that returns its element from `focusRoot()` has its focus kept by the stack.
    - Opening it moves focus into that element (made focusable with `tabindex="-1"`). A dialog drawn just after its push (`push` then `open`) gets focus once the caller's code has run.
    - Tab and Shift+Tab cycle its shown controls, unless the modal already called `preventDefault` for Tab (the character menu's tabs and panels, the merchant shop). A modal without a root still loses Tab's default.
    - When a modal leaves, the one beneath takes focus. When the last one leaves, focus returns to what had it before the first opened, unless something else has taken it since.
    - Roots today: the potion picker (its list, not the potion row), the character menu, the shop, choice, mastery choice and diagnostics.
    - Modals that hold focus on their own overlay (feedback, settings, command palette, message log) leave `focusRoot` out.
    - `src/ui/__tests__/modalStackFocus.test.ts` covers the rules, and `e2e/gameplay.spec.ts` covers the real dialogs.
  - **Registered:** every in-game modal. Character menu (`CharacterMenuModal`, `id = 'character-menu'`, the only home of the inventory, spellbook, bestiary, pacts, attribute allocation and the Rune of Return ranks), targeting, diagnostics, context help, the radial menu, the shop dialog (`ShopDialog`, `src/ui/shop/`, `id = 'shop'`: merchants and town services in the one dialog frame, registered by `InputHandler` when it opens), choice, mastery-perk choice, save & quit, settings/keybinds, save-code, feedback, the Rune of Return discovery modal, and the other-pack save warning (`showManifestMismatchDialog`, `id = 'manifest-mismatch'`) when a save is dropped onto the game in play. The feedback modal (`src/ui/feedbackModal.ts`) and the settings/keybind modal (`src/ui/settings/keybindModal.ts`) also open from the main menu, where `InputHandler` is disabled, so each takes keys through a `keydown` listener on its own focus-holding overlay element — never a second `window` listener — and stops their propagation, so each key reaches its `handleKeyDown` once; each pushes and removes its own single stack entry. That listener runs before `InputHandler`'s, which is what rebinding needs: the settings modal refocuses its overlay after re-rendering its keybind list, so the key to bind reaches it before `InputHandler` would claim F1/F2/F3 or the backquote. (Dwarven Winch and town-return modals were removed 2026-09-16 along with their underlying mechanics.)
  - **No accidental commits:** a level-up never opens a window: the header's points badge pulses and a toast (one per turn, however many levels arrive) names the points and the Character tab's key. Windows that do open mid-move (choice, mastery-perk choice) only stage a selection until Enter or a confirm button locks it in; attributes never take number keys. The shop, which opens inside the step that bumps its keeper, ignores the keys that trade or pay (numbers, Enter, Space, J, M, a service's offer key) as auto-repeats and for 250 ms after it opens, so a movement key held into a merchant buys and sells nothing. A level's points — attributes and Rune of Return ranks alike — are planned in `AttributeAllocationDraft` (`src/ui/attributeAllocationDraft.ts`) and reach the player only on Accept, through `Player.allocateAttribute` and `allocateRuneMastery`; Escape closes the menu without spending, and points locked in earlier can never be taken back.
  - **`UIModal` Normalization & Consolidated Character Menu:** Modals implement the `UIModal` interface (`id`, `isOpen`, `handleKeyDown(e): boolean`, `close()`). `CharacterMenuModal` (`src/ui/characterMenu/characterMenuModal.ts`, styled by `src/ui/styles/menu.css`) consolidates primary player screens into one tabbed shell that owns all the chrome (ADR-0011): the tab strip (key chip, label, a badge such as unspent points), the one close button, and a footer of the active tab's keys and actions, which a tab supplies through `MenuTab.footer()`; tabs draw only their content and decline the keys they don't use. Every tab fills the window less a 20px margin:
    1. **Inventory:** `InventoryTab` (`src/ui/inventory/inventoryTab.ts`, styled by `inventory.css`) draws four panels: the paperdoll with every slot named, the backpack (filters, sort, coins, and what the gear weighs in kg), the ground or an open container (breadcrumbs) or the companion's pack (K), and the selected item with its actions. The hero's stats are on the Character tab only. Its state and rules are `InventoryController` (`inventoryController.ts`, no DOM): selection and focus through `ItemInspector`, container navigation, drag-and-drop targets, the right-click menu, splitting a stack, and the keys (arrows inside a panel, Tab between panels, Enter, E/U/D/T/P/G/Y/K/C/O/J, 1-9; Esc backs out of a menu, a selection, the companion view or a container before the menu closes). Item names take their color from `itemTone()` as `--ui-rarity-*`/`--ui-coin-*` role tokens, and an identified item of a family (or an artifact) is framed in the same color (N29, `itemFrameClass`): its cell, its paperdoll slot, the shop's and the item panel's icons, and its square on the ground (`itemFrameColor`, `src/rendering/itemFrame.ts`); a legend under the paperdoll names them. A double-click on a container on or beside the hero's tile opens it here (`CanvasRenderer.onOpenContainer`).
    2. **Character:** identity and vitals, the only allocation UI (S/D/C/I plan a point, Shift+letter removes one, −/+ buttons, previews from the engine's formulas, an "If you accept" before→after list) with attribute milestones, and the Rune of Return ranks (1/2/3), which spend the same points (`src/ui/characterMenu/characterTab.ts`). E, U, the `allocate-stats` command and the header's points button open it (a level-up only toasts); every "rune tree" entry point (Shift+T, the discovery dialog, the inventory, the rune-smith) opens it at the ranks.
    3. **Spellbook:** spells known, the selected spell's details and quickbar slot, and the grimoire grid with how sealed slots open, a lit link between slots whose spells shape each other (`GrimoireMatrixManager.slotInteractions`), and a line saying what the slot under the pointer or focus does (`spellbookTab.ts`); Enter casts.
    4. **Bestiary:** every creature, known ones first, knowledge-rank tags (Seen, Slain, Studied at `SPECIES_MASTERY_KILLS`) with a legend, the family's mastery perks and kill rites (`bestiaryTab.ts`). Species kills only fill the page; a family's mastery (`MonsterCategoryDefinition.masteryKills`) offers the perk (Q7 "A"): the shared five and the pack's own for that family (`masteryPerkOptions`), in the Bestiary and in `MasteryChoiceModal`.
    5. **Pacts:** pacts as cost/reward cards and what the sealed ones add up to (`pactsTab.ts`).
    6. **Story:** `StoryTab` (`src/ui/characterMenu/storyTab.ts`, model in `storyModel.ts`): the Descent line, the Saga (objective, deeds done, locked deeds as riddles, the Chronicle of discovery events), and lore and standing (Left/Right; sealed pacts are the Pacts tab's). See content-quests-and-triggers.md, Story Presentation.
    - **Dependency Inversion & Engine Purity:** The shell consumes tabs through the `MenuTab` interface (`src/ui/characterMenu/menuTab.ts`). `src/ui/` only imports types from `src/rendering/`, so tabs that show sprites (the inventory) take a `drawItemIcon` callback from `src/main.ts`.
    - **Hotkeys & Navigation:** Tabs cycle via `Tab` / `Shift+Tab`; a tab that claims Tab (the inventory's panels) keeps it until it reaches its edge, and Shift+Tab enters such a tab on its last panel. Each tab's action keys (`inventory` I, `character_menu` E, `cast_spell` Z, `compendium` B, `pact` P, `story` O by default) open it from the game via `InputHandler.toggleCharacterMenu(tabId)`, and switch to it inside the menu once the active tab declines the key; the shell reads them from the player's keybindings (`setKeyResolver`), so chips and keys follow rebinding. The active tab's own key closes the menu.
  - **Still intercepted inline by `InputHandler`:** the map and inspect overlays. Right after modal-stack routing, an open map receives every key (`MapOverlay.handleKeyDown` closes on M/Esc/Space, pages floors, and absorbs the rest), so nothing reaches the simulation beneath it; the inspect overlay is checked the same way before other keys dispatch. This is equivalent to stack gating for a single non-nesting overlay, and `mapOverlayInput.test.ts` covers it.
  - **Screen-level, not modals:** `InputHandler.enabled` remains for whole-screen transitions — entering the game, returning to the main menu, the game-over screen, and the title/saga screens. Those are not modals over a live simulation, so the stack does not apply. Dialogs that open only from those screens — Load a saved game (`SaveSlotModal`), the hall of fame — take keys through a `keydown` listener on their own focus-holding overlay (Escape closes) and refocus it after a redraw. Deleting a save, there or from the title roster, asks through `showConfirmDialog` (`src/ui/confirmDialog.ts`: the dialog frame on the system layer, focus-holding, Escape cancels), never the browser's `confirm()`.
- **HUD Bars & Viewport Scaling:** the viewport/letterboxing behavior comes from a 2026-09-16 overhaul ([ADR-0004](../decisions/0004-hud-overhaul-retrospective.md)); the HUD layout itself has since changed. Current state: `ViewportManager.recalculate()` (`src/rendering/viewport.ts`) subtracts the real mounted height of every HUD bar (a bar nested in another listed bar counts once) and the width of side columns (`SIDE_COLUMN_IDS`: the combat sidebar), and re-runs on a `ResizeObserver`. The map column (`#game-container`: header, canvas, console, log) is as wide as the letterboxed canvas but at least 1024px while the window has the room, with the canvas centred in it, so a short window (1366x768) still gives the console's text its width. Above the map sits the hero square (name, depth, level with an experience bar, renown; a pulsing badge when attribute points are unspent) beside two bars: screens and system (Bestiary, Cmds, Help, Feedback, Dev, `☰ [Esc] Menu`) over actions (Inv, Cast, Look, Map, Rest, Wait, Search, Stairs). Buttons collapse via `@container` queries instead of wrapping. The canvas reserves no strip: map/look/aim/grimoire controls draw as a pill over the bottom of the map while that mode is open. Below the map: the action console and the message log as its last row; the log's label at its top right, or the bindable `message_log` key (Shift+M), opens its history (`src/ui/messageLogModal.ts`, a modal reading every line the engine holds: 150, of which a save keeps 100). The console holds, left to right, the health orb, the potion row, the context action button (`src/ui/console/`: the one thing worth doing here, in priority order attack an adjacent hostile, take loot underfoot, use stairs, talk, open a door, rest when hurt with no hostile in sight and resting allowed (`AutoRestManager.restRefusal`: not while a timed event counts down or a prologue runs, whose turn-count clocks would run out in the sleep), close a door; `F` or a click), the spell belt with the pack's current objective under it (`getCurrentObjective`, plus a bearing: to the nearest entity the objective's `pointTo` names while any is on the floor, else the nearest known stairs down; dropped by a container query when the belt's column is too narrow), a tray of chips shown only while relevant (Rune of Return charges or channeling, overflow debt, sealed pacts) with the companion card beneath, and the mana orb. `ViewportManager` also observes each bar's own size, so a console that grows re-fits the canvas. There is no turn counter on the HUD.
- **F1 context help (`src/ui/help/contextHelp.ts`):** one card for what the player is looking at: map, shop, look, aim, inventory, the opening (while the pack's prologue runs: its keys, not the town's barred shops), town or exploration from the overlays and floor, and before those the composition root's `setScreenContext`: the altar card (its rite open, or an altar beside the hero; rites and glyphs), the Story card (that tab open), the Spellbook card (that tab open; the grimoire's rules in the pack's words), the Bestiary, Character and Pacts cards (that tab open), the Rune of Return card (its discovery, or a channel under way).
- **Combat sidebar (`src/ui/sidebar/`):** a column beside the map at full HUD height, replacing the old widescreen flank panels (what they showed lives on in the character menu's Story tab; packs no longer declare a `flankLayout`). Sections: a minimap (`CanvasRenderer.drawMinimap`, click for the full map), hostile monsters in sight (`Player.isHostileTo`, as resting judges them: a neutral is left out) nearest first with HP and any wind-up, the hero's conditions with turns left (statuses, sensing spells, story countdowns, encumbrance), and ground items in sight with what the hero stands on. `sidebarModel.ts` computes each section without the DOM. Pointing at a monster links both ways: hovering a row calls `CanvasRenderer.pointAtTile` (brackets and card on the map), and the renderer's `onFocusEntityChanged` (mouse hover or look reticle) highlights the row. Under the ground list, while one waits, sits a first-time hint (`src/ui/hints/`): the pack's note on a system (`manifest.firstTimeHints`) the first time this hero meets it, never modal, until dismissed; more queue behind it. `hintModel.ts` decides what is met: an altar reached or a kill rite performed (events); a companion at the hero's side, renown earned, a Rune of Return carried, a faction standing moved from its start, the first saga deed, a creature with a kill rite in sight outside the pack's prologue, two spells known below town in a pack with a grimoire (state after each turn); the pact keeper greeted (`ShopDialog.onGreet`). Shown hints are `hint:<id>` in `Player.tutorialFlags`, saved with the hero; the "First-time hints" setting turns them off.
- **Action cues (`src/ui/hints/actionCues.ts`):** while the pack's prologue runs (`manifest.prologue`), the HUD slot for the move the moment calls for glows, with its key and verb in a chip above it (`cueMark.ts`, `.hud-cue`): the potion-row slot of a healing potion when the hero is at 40% HP or less, and the quick-spell slot of an affordable ray spell when a hostile in sight, two or more tiles off and within range, is fleeing or at half HP or less. Never a dialog or a log line; the glow goes out when the moment passes. `findActionCues` decides from state after each turn; `PotionRow.setCue`/`QuickSpellsBar.setCue` keep the mark through redraws. The "First-time hints" setting turns them off too.
- **Controls note (`src/ui/controlsPrimer.ts`):** the one dialog before a new hero's first step (not on Continue or a load): moving, diagonals (number pad and arrow chording), fighting, waiting, the spell and potion keys from the player's bindings, one compact block on the mouse (click a distant tile to walk, point to see), the log and its history key, and F3, and where Settings is. It is on the modal stack, so it holds the keys until answered; "See every key" opens Settings. Its own tick, or the "Controls reminder" setting, stops it.
