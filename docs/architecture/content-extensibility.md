## Declarative Manifests
Each content pack exports a `GameContentManifest` (`src/engine/types/manifest.ts`). The `GameEngine` constructor registers its entries, for example: `monsters`, `spells`, `traps`, `statusEffects`/`statusHandlers`, `aiBehaviors`/`aiStrategies`, `actionHooks`, `affinityMatrix`, `pacts`, `progressionConfig`, `initialWorldState`, `quest`, and `theme`. Content assembles behavior from composable primitives: status afflictions, spawners, stat triggers, damage affinities, drop tables, and quest arcs. A status handler marked `affliction` (the built-in poison, paralysis, slow, blindness and stun; cotw's burning) is one a cure of every affliction, a potion's `cure_status: 'all'`, purges; an unmarked status (haste, a channel, a floor's darkness) stays.

Presentation data rides along: `branding`/`town.name` supply shared screens' pack wording (including `branding.xpName`, the experience term the HUD, log and memorial use, "XP" when absent; and `branding.manaName`/`manaUnit`, the spell resource's name and the unit amounts are written in, resolved by `resolveManaTerms`, "Mana"/"MP" when absent), and `firstTimeHints` gives a short note per system the HUD introduces once per hero (`FirstTimeHintId`: altar, killRite, pactKeeper, companion, renown, runeOfReturn, factionStanding, story, grimoire; `{key:<action>}` in its text names the bound key; a system without one shows no hint). A pack's art (`spriteRecipes`, `atlas.terrain`, `atlas.overlays`) and its item families (`itemFamilies`) are in [content-items-and-art.md](content-items-and-art.md).

## Two Hook Mechanisms
1. **Action hooks** (`manifest.actionHooks`, interface `ActionHook` in `actionPipeline.ts`): priority-ordered `pre`/`post` hooks around pipeline-executed actions. They receive `ActionHookContext` (`action`, `engine`, `actionType`, and `result` in the post phase). A pre-hook can short-circuit with its own `ActionResult`. Hooks fire for every actor; `ActionHookContext.actor` identifies who is acting, so content that should react only to the player compares it against `engine.player`.
2. **Declarative event hooks** (`HookDispatcher` in `src/engine/hooks/hookDispatcher.ts`): `HookDescriptor` data (event, chance, predicate, primitive action) attached to items and monsters. They receive `HookContext` (`engine`, `attacker`, `defender`, `damage`, …). The action is one of the engine's built-in primitives (`ActionPrimitive`: `applyStatus`, `spawnSurface`, `spawnGas`, `heal`, `bonusDamage`); content cannot register another, and there are no global hooks. Every way a monster is made from its definition (floor, vault, wandering and boss spawns, and a load) carries the definition's `hooks`. `onTurnStart` fires at the start of an awake monster's turn, after its status tick, unless it is stunned or paralyzed; its `defender` is the entity the monster engages when that one is within 8 tiles and in line of sight. A primitive aimed at `target: 'target'` with no one there is skipped rather than landing on the hook's owner. `onHit` (the attacker's hooks) and `onDamageTaken` (the defender's, when wounded) fire for every blow with an attacker: melee, spell damage, chain-lightning hops and monster wind-ups (`dispatchDamageHooks`, `src/engine/hooks/damageHooks.ts`), before the blow's death resolves; not for a blow the defender was immune to or absorbed. `onBlock` is melee's alone.

## Injected Context
Handlers get the live engine through an injected context object rather than importing engine runtime state. Both context types expose `EngineContext` (`src/engine/types/engineContext.ts`), not the raw `GameEngine`: `player`, `map`, `surfaces`, `worldState`, the seeded `rng`, `log()`, the world-flag accessors, `currentFloor`, `removeEntity()` (those two added so a pack's hooks can scope an encounter to its floor and take its NPCs off the map through the scheduler-aware path, not `map.removeEntity`), `addEntity()` (added for tracker 5.3 so a pack can bring a monster onto the floor mid-visit, into the scheduler too: cotw's dark floors draw monsters out of the dark), and `compendium` narrowed to `getEntry` (added so a pack can answer a kind of death, however it happened: an action's own result does not yet carry its events when post-hooks run). `GameEngine` satisfies it structurally, so nothing is wrapped at runtime and the narrowing is enforced by the type checker — a handler reaching for `engine.scheduler` no longer compiles. Widen it deliberately: each member added becomes part of the content-facing contract. The dispatcher's own built-in primitives (damage, death, statuses, surfaces) are engine code rather than handlers and narrow back through a single documented cast.

## Content Registries
Manifest registration writes into per-engine registries owned by `GameEngine` via `engine.registries` (`EngineRegistries`). Content registries (monsters, traps, spells, companions, AI strategies, AI behaviors, status handlers, tiles) are seeded from process defaults upon engine creation and supplemented by manifest registration, so two engines built from different manifests do not collide. Runtime state registries (`containers`, `itemIndex`) are per-engine and isolated, populated dynamically from the live world state rather than seeded from process defaults. Module-level facades (e.g. `MonsterRegistry`, `SpellRegistry`, `CompanionRegistry`, `StatusHandlerRegistry`, the tile lookups `getTileDefinition`/`hasTileDefinition`, `containerRegistry`, `itemIndex`) forward into whichever engine's store is active (`activateRegistries(engine.registries)`), holding no state maps of their own, so each engine maintains independent lookups. Traps have no facade: an engine's trap definitions are `engine.registries.traps`, and the trap generator places traps from `manifest.traps`.

## Pack-Neutral Presentation
The rule is ARCHITECTURE.md §3: `src/ui/`, `src/rendering/` and `src/main/**` name no pack. This is where each kind of pack-specific presentation comes from.
- **Wording:** a pack's title, tagline, town and flavor come from the manifest (`name`, `description`, `town.name`, `branding`).
- **Sprites and icons:** art comes from `spriteRecipes`. A recipe keyed by a monster or item definition ID, or by an NPC's id, is that entity's sprite. One keyed `ui~<name>` is that UI icon: the vocabulary is `src/ui/icons.ts`, DOM shows it as `ui-icon` elements baked at startup by `installUiIcons`, canvas overlays draw it through `canvasIcons.ts`, and an icon the pack doesn't draw doesn't show. One keyed `spell~<spellId>`, else `spell~<elementId>`, is a spell's rune (`src/ui/spellRunes.ts`, baked with the icons); a spell's color is its element's in `affinityMatrix`, else its own `visual.color`. Presentation, the F2 developer diagnostics included, shows these icons, not emoji.
- **Terrain and overlays:** neighbour-aware terrain, torchlight and remembered cells come from `atlas.terrain`, and surfaces and gas from `atlas.overlays` ([content-items-and-art.md](content-items-and-art.md)). `src/rendering/` applies them generically and names no zone or floor band; a type the pack draws no art for shows as a neutral wash in role colors, never as nothing.
- **Theme tokens:** color, type, radius and fonts come from the semantic role tokens of the pack's `ThemeTokens`. `resolveThemeTokens`/`applyThemeTokens` in `src/rendering/theme.ts` write them as `--ui-*` variables; the static type, space, layer and motion scales are in `src/ui/styles/tokens.css` ([ADR-0011](../decisions/0011-menus-and-visual-system-direction.md)). Presentation names roles (`var(--ui-accent)`, `theme.textMuted`), never hues.
- **Text size and UI scale:** canvas UI text is sized with `uiFont()`, which holds the 11px floor at every window size (map-tile glyphs and cell badges scale with the cell instead). The player's UI scale (`src/ui/uiScale.ts`, Auto by default) multiplies both: it zooms the interface's DOM roots (never the map canvas, which fits to what they leave) and `uiFont()`'s sizes alike (ADR-0011, 2026-10-04 amendment).
