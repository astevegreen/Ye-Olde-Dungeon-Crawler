Explanatory only — every binding rule about content integration lives on the guaranteed-read path (`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s §3 stub and `content-extensibility.md`'s core sections), never only here. ## Milestone Renown Ledger
`manifest.renownMilestones` (`RenownMilestoneDefinition[]`) declares per-category, cumulative renown milestones (one-time or repeatable); `manifest.renownTitles` declares title thresholds per category or across the total. `src/engine/renown/renownLedger.ts`'s `recordMilestone(engine, id)` is called at generic engine trigger points (`UncurseAction` on cleansing a curse, `SearchAction` on finding a secret door) — a no-op if no milestone with that ID is defined, the same "engine calls a generic hook, content may or may not react" pattern as `emitDiscovery`. Renown is per-character: it rides entirely on the existing `WorldState.counters`/`flags` (`renown:<category>` counters, `renown_milestone_earned:<id>` flags), which already serialize with the save — no `SaveData` field or migration step was needed. Vendor unlocks need no new predicate type either: `Predicate`'s existing `minCounter` variant, evaluated against a `renown:<category>` counter, already gates a `MerchantConfig`/`Item.predicate`-filtered stock item (see `Merchant.getAvailableStock`, now actually consulted by `ShopOverlay.getBuyableItems` — previously present but unused by the live UI). The active title is surfaced in the character header (`getActiveTitle(engine)`).

## Tag-Filtered Radial Auras
`MonsterDefinition.tags?: string[]` (wired through `createScaledMonster`/`Monster.createFromDefinition`) sets tags resolved at runtime via the pre-existing `Entity.tags`/`hasTag()` (which already also matches faction and entity type). `findTaggedEntitiesInRadius` (`src/engine/combat/radialAuraFilter.ts`) is the generic, reusable piece: a bounded-radius (§6-style, no unbounded scans), any-of-`tags` entity query, shared by two call sites — `HookDispatcher`'s `radialAuraFilter` `ActionPrimitive` (applies a nested primitive to every match; item/monster hooks) and `DrinkPotionAction`'s `radial_status` consumable effect (applies a status to every match; potions/deployables). Tag matching is a flat any-of-`tags` list, not a boolean `and`/`or`/`not` expression tree — simpler than originally designed and consistent with how `hasTag()` is already used elsewhere (e.g. `combat.ts`'s damage-affinity bonuses).

## Level-Scaled Elemental Resistance
`resolveLevelScaledResistance(curve, level)`/`applyLevelScaledElementalMitigation(amount, level, curve)` (`stats/levelScaledResistance.ts`) resolve a numeric mitigation fraction from an ascending `{level, resistance}` step curve — the first real consumer of `calculateAttribute`'s long-reserved but previously-unused `'elementalResistance'` attribute key. Declared per-element on `ProgressionConfig.elementalResistanceCurve`. Deliberately *not* wired into `Entity.takeElementalDamage`/the categorical `elementalResistances` affinity system combat already uses (untouched, so this can't regress it). Its original consumer was removed and replaced by a different mechanic — see [ADR-0003](../decisions/0003-jarnvidr-exposure-handler-to-giant-blood.md). The module remains as unused-but-legitimate generic engine capability; no other content pack consumes it today.

## Zone-Tiered, Difficulty-Scaled Monster Power
`types/monsterScaling.ts`, `dungeon/spawner.ts` (2026-09-18): replaces `cotw`'s old flat per-floor scaling curve with a step function over content-declared `MonsterPowerTier[]` breakpoints (`{floor, multiplier}`, ascending, same step-curve idiom as Level-Scaled Elemental Resistance above), combined with a difficulty's two knobs — `basePowerMultiplier` (a uniform shift, including at the first tier) and `scalingRateMultiplier` (scales only the tier curve's growth *above* 1.0) — plus an optional `bossPowerFloorMultiplier` that floors a `boss`/`miniboss`-tagged `MonsterDefinition`'s multiplier at `tier * bossPowerFloorMultiplier`, so a low-difficulty base power can't trivialize a guarded fight. `GameContentManifest.monsterScaling?: MonsterScalingConfig` is optional; `scaleMonsterStats`/`createScaledMonster`/`populateDungeonFloor` fall back to the original flat curve byte-for-byte when it's omitted (e.g. `warcraft`, which doesn't set it), so this is purely additive. `cotw`'s own `COTW_MONSTER_SCALING` (`content/cotw/monsterScaling.ts`) ties its seven tiers to the real zone `minFloor` bands (Rime Hollows, Abandoned Dwarven Works, Obsidian Siphon, Tarnished Silver Veins, World Bark Descent, Maw of Malice, the floor-50 climax) and climbs continuously across the Act 1 → Act 2 boundary — no reset, since Act 2 is pure escalating monster power with no thematic tie-in of its own. Threading `difficulty` (a `Player`/save concept) down to where floors are actually generated required two additive trailing-argument changes at `engine.ts`'s `changeFloor()` (the real per-floor generation call) and its `diagnostics.spawnMonster` triage tool — both minimal, no new logic added to `engine.ts` itself (§8.1 exception 4, owner-authorized; made before exception 4 was written down — see [ADR-0005](../decisions/0005-owner-authorized-exception-and-agent-workflow.md)).

## Permanent Stat Modification & Companion Grants
`ChoiceConsequence` (`types/choice.ts`) gained `modifyPermanentStat` (adjusts `Player.attack`/`defense`'s *raw base* — via `baseAttackValue`/`baseDefenseValue`, not the equipment-inclusive getter, which would double-count gear bonuses on every future equip/unequip — so a story choice can permanently reshape a build) and `grantCompanion` (bonds the acquisition-gate flag and calls `summonCompanion`, so a story beat can grant a pet without a trainer visit). The consequence-interpreter loop was factored out of `ExecuteChoiceAction` into an exported `applyConsequences(consequences, engine, player)` so other triggers — see [content-quests-and-triggers.md](content-quests-and-triggers.md) — reuse the exact same, tested semantics instead of a second interpreter.

## Per-Zone Room Decoration & Floor Hazard Advisories
`manifest.roomDecoration` (`RoomDecorationBand[]`) tunes `RoomDecorator` per floor band: puddle and chasm-fissure chances, grand-hall and pillar chances, and how often interior partitions are iron bars. The first band containing the floor applies; with no band the engine uses neutral defaults (no puddles or fissures, colonnades 0.6, pillars 0.5, wall partitions). `manifest.floorHazards` (`FloorHazardAdvisory[]`) gives the run advisor the element to warn about per floor band, with pack-authored title, message (`{floor}` substituted) and recommendation; with none, the advisor issues no elemental warnings. Both replaced floor ranges and zone flavor text that were hard-coded in the engine; `cotw` declares its zones in `src/content/cotw/floorBands.ts`, reproducing the previous generation exactly. The fallback monster spawner (`DungeonGenerator` with `spawnMonsters: true`) likewise draws from the pack's monster candidates, or the registry, rather than a fixed list of monster ids.

## Per-Zone Floor Layouts
`manifest.floorLayouts` (`FloorLayoutBand[]`) picks a layout strategy per floor band, with tuning in `params`; a quest's explicit `floorGenerators[floor]` still wins, and floors outside every band use the quest's `defaultGenerator` (else `bsp`). `manifest.floorSize` sets procedural floor dimensions (default 50x35); rooms-and-corridors scales its room count with the area.

Besides `bsp` and `cavern`, `DungeonGeneratorRegistry` holds six character-grid strategies (`src/engine/dungeon/layout/`): `caverns` (cellular caves whose pockets are stitched in with tunnels rather than deleted; optional lake, pools, pits, pillar groves), `halls` (a symmetric plan: a colonnaded great hall inside a 2-wide ring road with rooms on every side; optional central pit), `rift` (caves split end to end by a chasm river with banks and 2–4 bridges; the stairs are on the far bank), `lattice` (straight drifts on a grid with chambers at junctions and dead-end drifts), `warrens` (chambers joined by a 2-wide spanning-tree backbone plus looping side tunnels; optional pillar grove around a pool) and `spine` (a spine passage with curved rib chambers; optional pool chamber and chasm pits). Parameter keys are documented on each draft in `drafts.ts`; `landmarkVaultIds` stamps one of the listed vaults on every floor of the band.

A draft only shapes the grid. `LayoutStrategy` (`layoutStrategy.ts`) owns the guarantees every layout keeps: the forced vault (`forcedVaultId`) and 1–2 random eligible vaults stamp through `VaultStamper`, with connectors carved out to the network and vault interiors never carved; every walkable cell outside a vault joins the network (secret caches through their secret door); the down stairs are the farthest floor tile reachable without secrets; the spawn is never in a vault or next to a hazard; and `rooms` is `RectRoom[]` with the spawn room first (built rooms, or virtual rooms cut from organic floor on a coarse grid). A draft that can't meet these after eight attempts falls back to rooms and corridors. Every choice draws from the floor's seed. `layoutStrategies.test.ts` checks all of this across seeds for each strategy, including the floor-5, -22 and -45 forced vaults.

`cotw` uses one strategy per zone (`src/content/cotw/floorLayouts.ts`) on 57x40 floors: `caverns` with a black-ice mere and a Draugr Barrow landmark (1–9), `halls` with the forge pit (10–17), `rift` with the Siphon Pylon landmark (18–25), `lattice` with a sump (26–33), `warrens` with a knothole grove (34–42) and `spine` with the eye pool (43–49). Its forced vaults (floor 5 rune chamber, floor 22 Siphon Altar, floor 45 fang vault) stamp through each strategy; `zoneLayouts.test.ts` checks them per zone.

The boss floor is `DungeonArc.generateBossLair`. When the quest's `bossFloorLayout.layout` is set (rows of tile characters, with an optional `legend` for pack tiles), the lair is drawn from it; the boss, its four guards and its hoard keep the built-in hall's arrangement around `bossSpawn`. `cotw`'s lair is the Heartwood (`lair.ts`).

The town (floor 0) is `TownMapGenerator`. By default it is an open courtyard with each of `town.buildings` walled in. When `town.layout` is set (rows: `#` rock or wall, `.` ground, `+`/`'` doors, `>` stairs down, and a `legend` from other characters to the pack's tile types), the town is drawn from the rows and takes their size; `buildings` then only name the interiors (merchants, and the renderer's `town_<buildingType>` look). Legend tiles resolve from the pack's `tiles` before an engine exists (a new character starting in town) and from the tile registry after.

`cotw`'s Bjarnarhaven (`townLayout.ts`) is a 56x36 clearing in the pines, built from shapes and a fixed seed: cobbled lanes wind from the main street to every door and the cellar stairs, a plaza holds a frozen fountain, and statues, carts, market stalls, barrels and woodpiles are the pack's own impassable tiles. Any open cell the lanes don't reach becomes woods. `townLayout.test.ts` checks that every NPC, door and the stairs are reachable and each shopkeeper stands inside their building.
