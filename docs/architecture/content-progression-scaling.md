# Progression & Scaling

> Topic doc under [content-extensibility.md](content-extensibility.md) ([ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) §3). Explanatory only — every binding rule about content integration lives on the guaranteed-read path (`ARCHITECTURE.md`'s §3 stub and `content-extensibility.md`'s core sections), never only here. Flag a conflict rather than resolving it here (§8.2).

## Milestone Renown Ledger
`manifest.renownMilestones` (`RenownMilestoneDefinition[]`) declares per-category, cumulative renown milestones (one-time or repeatable); `manifest.renownTitles` declares title thresholds per category or across the total. `src/engine/renown/renownLedger.ts`'s `recordMilestone(engine, id)` is called at generic engine trigger points (`UncurseAction` and the temple's `temple_cleanse` command on breaking a curse, `SearchAction` on finding a secret door) — a no-op if no milestone with that ID is defined, the same "engine calls a generic hook, content may or may not react" pattern as `emitDiscovery`. A choice records one with the `recordMilestone` consequence; content code, which reaches the engine through `EngineContext` (no manifest), awards its own definition with `awardMilestone(ctx, def)`. Renown granted any other way (a bare `modifyCounter` on `renown:<category>`) skips the total the HUD shows, so content doesn't. Renown is per-character: it rides entirely on the existing `WorldState.counters`/`flags` (`renown:<category>` counters, `renown_milestone_earned:<id>` flags), which already serialize with the save — no `SaveData` field or migration step was needed. Vendor unlocks need no new predicate type either: `Predicate`'s existing `minCounter` variant, evaluated against a `renown:<category>` counter, already gates a `MerchantConfig`/`Item.predicate`-filtered stock item (see `Merchant.getAvailableStock`, now actually consulted by `ShopDialog.getBuyableItems` (`src/ui/shop/shopDialog.ts`) — previously present but unused by the live UI). The active title is surfaced in the character header (`getActiveTitle(engine)`).

## Tag-Filtered Radial Auras
`MonsterDefinition.tags?: string[]` (wired through `createScaledMonster`/`Monster.createFromDefinition`) sets tags resolved at runtime via the pre-existing `Entity.tags`/`hasTag()` (which already also matches faction and entity type). `findTaggedEntitiesInRadius` (`src/engine/combat/radialAuraFilter.ts`) is the generic, reusable piece: a bounded-radius (§6-style, no unbounded scans), any-of-`tags` entity query, shared by two call sites — `HookDispatcher`'s `radialAuraFilter` `ActionPrimitive` (applies a nested primitive to every match; item/monster hooks) and `DrinkPotionAction`'s `radial_status` consumable effect (applies a status to every match; potions/deployables). Its `release_gas` effect fills the user's tile and the open tiles within a radius with a gas (cotw's Bellows-Skin Canteen: dense steam over radius 2 for 5 turns, beside its blinding `radial_status`), which hides whoever stands in it from monsters (`ai/perception.ts`). Tag matching is a flat any-of-`tags` list, not a boolean `and`/`or`/`not` expression tree — simpler than originally designed and consistent with how `hasTag()` is already used elsewhere (e.g. `combat.ts`'s damage-affinity bonuses).

## Level-Scaled Elemental Resistance
`resolveLevelScaledResistance(curve, level)`/`applyLevelScaledElementalMitigation(amount, level, curve)` (`stats/levelScaledResistance.ts`) resolve a numeric mitigation fraction from an ascending `{level, resistance}` step curve — the first real consumer of `calculateAttribute`'s long-reserved but previously-unused `'elementalResistance'` attribute key. Declared per-element on `ProgressionConfig.elementalResistanceCurve`. Deliberately *not* wired into `Entity.takeElementalDamage`/the categorical `elementalResistances` affinity system combat already uses (untouched, so this can't regress it). Its original consumer was removed and replaced by a different mechanic — see [ADR-0003](../decisions/0003-jarnvidr-exposure-handler-to-giant-blood.md). The module remains as unused-but-legitimate generic engine capability; no other content pack consumes it today.

## Zone-Tiered, Difficulty-Scaled Monster Power
`types/monsterScaling.ts`, `dungeon/spawner.ts` (2026-09-18): replaces `cotw`'s old flat per-floor scaling curve with a step function over content-declared `MonsterPowerTier[]` breakpoints (`{floor, multiplier}`, ascending, same step-curve idiom as Level-Scaled Elemental Resistance above), combined with a difficulty's two knobs — `basePowerMultiplier` (a uniform shift, including at the first tier) and `scalingRateMultiplier` (scales only the tier curve's growth *above* 1.0) — plus an optional `bossPowerFloorMultiplier` that floors a `boss`/`miniboss`-tagged `MonsterDefinition`'s multiplier at `tier * bossPowerFloorMultiplier`, so a low-difficulty base power can't trivialize a guarded fight. HP and attack take the whole multiplier; defense takes it raised to `defenseExponent` (default 1, the whole multiplier). `cotw` sets 0.5, defense by the square root (owner, 2026-10-07; R-cotw-17): Níðhögg's ×7 on floor 50 (medium) gives defense ×2.6, 37 rather than 98, because a hero's melee grows by flat weapon steps a full-multiplier defense outran. A monster's XP follows the zone tier alone (Q4 "B", Q25, 2026-10-04): the difficulty knobs and the boss guard raise the fight, not its reward, so a full clear is worth the same on every difficulty. `GameContentManifest.monsterScaling?: MonsterScalingConfig` is optional; `scaleMonsterStats`/`createScaledMonster`/`populateDungeonFloor` fall back to the original flat curve byte-for-byte when it's omitted (e.g. `warcraft`, which doesn't set it), so this is purely additive. `cotw`'s own `COTW_MONSTER_SCALING` (`content/cotw/monsterScaling.ts`) ties its seven tiers to the real zone `minFloor` bands (Rime Hollows, Abandoned Dwarven Works, Obsidian Siphon, Tarnished Silver Veins, World Bark Descent, Maw of Malice, the floor-50 climax) and climbs continuously across the Act 1 → Act 2 boundary — no reset, since Act 2 is pure escalating monster power with no thematic tie-in of its own. Threading `difficulty` (a `Player`/save concept) down to where floors are actually generated required two additive trailing-argument changes at `engine.ts`'s `changeFloor()` (the real per-floor generation call) and its `diagnostics.spawnMonster` triage tool — both minimal, no new logic added to `engine.ts` itself (§8.1 exception 4, owner-authorized; made before exception 4 was written down — see [ADR-0005](../decisions/0005-owner-authorized-exception-and-agent-workflow.md)).

## Permanent Stat Modification & Companion Grants
`ChoiceConsequence` (`types/choice.ts`) gained `modifyPermanentStat` (adjusts `Player.attack`/`defense`'s *raw base* — via `baseAttackValue`/`baseDefenseValue`, not the equipment-inclusive getter, which would double-count gear bonuses on every future equip/unequip — so a story choice can permanently reshape a build) and `grantCompanion` (bonds the acquisition-gate flag and calls `summonCompanion`, so a story beat can grant a pet without a trainer visit), `learnSpell` (teaches a spell by id; one already known is left alone), and `cureStatus` (ends the listed statuses on the hero, e.g. cotw's Amber Root Fire clearing blindness and slowness). The consequence-interpreter loop was factored out of `ExecuteChoiceAction` into an exported `applyConsequences(consequences, engine, player)` so other triggers — see [content-quests-and-triggers.md](content-quests-and-triggers.md) — reuse the exact same, tested semantics instead of a second interpreter.

## Per-Zone Room Decoration & Floor Hazard Advisories
`manifest.roomDecoration` (`RoomDecorationBand[]`) tunes `RoomDecorator` per floor band: puddle and chasm-fissure chances, grand-hall and pillar chances, and how often interior partitions are iron bars. The first band containing the floor applies; with no band the engine uses neutral defaults (no puddles or fissures, colonnades 0.6, pillars 0.5, wall partitions). `manifest.floorHazards` (`FloorHazardAdvisory[]`) gives the run advisor the element to warn about per floor band, with pack-authored title, message (`{floor}` substituted) and recommendation; with none, the advisor issues no elemental warnings. Both replaced floor ranges and zone flavor text that were hard-coded in the engine; `cotw` declares its zones in `src/content/cotw/floorBands.ts`, reproducing the previous generation exactly. The fallback monster spawner (`DungeonGenerator` with `spawnMonsters: true`) likewise draws from the pack's monster candidates, or the registry, rather than a fixed list of monster ids.

Every random monster draw (floor population, vault fill, wandering spawns) goes through `selectDungeonMonsterDefinition` (`dungeon/spawner.ts`): eligible are monsters not `placedOnly` with `minFloor` at or below the floor, each weighted by `dungeonSpawnWeight`, 1 on the floor it unlocks and falling by the cube of its age (half six floors on, about a tenth at twelve). A floor's mix is led by its own stretch of the dungeon and no one monster takes a quarter of the draws however few unlock nearby; it replaced a draw that gave 75% of picks to whichever monsters unlocked last, which flooded floors where one unlocked alone (2026-10-02). A vault's `preferredMonsters` hold its `M` spots only while the drawn guard's weight is at least 0.1 (about twelve floors past its unlock); past that the spot draws from the floor, so an open-ended shallow vault met deep keeps its shape but not its rats.

## Per-Zone Floor Layouts
`manifest.floorLayouts` (`FloorLayoutBand[]`) picks a layout strategy per floor band, with tuning in `params`; a quest's explicit `floorGenerators[floor]` still wins, and floors outside every band use the quest's `defaultGenerator` (else `bsp`). `manifest.floorSize` sets procedural floor dimensions (default 50x35); rooms-and-corridors scales its room count with the area.

Besides `bsp` and `cavern`, `DungeonGeneratorRegistry` holds six character-grid strategies (`src/engine/dungeon/layout/`): `caverns` (cellular caves whose pockets are stitched in with tunnels rather than deleted; optional lake, pools, pits, pillar groves), `halls` (a symmetric plan: a colonnaded great hall inside a 2-wide ring road with rooms on every side; optional central pit), `rift` (caves split end to end by a chasm river with banks and 2–4 bridges; the stairs are on the far bank), `lattice` (straight drifts on a grid with chambers at junctions and dead-end drifts), `warrens` (chambers joined by a 2-wide spanning-tree backbone plus looping side tunnels; optional pillar grove around a pool) and `spine` (a spine passage with curved rib chambers; optional pool chamber and chasm pits). Parameter keys are documented on each draft in `drafts.ts`; `landmarkVaultIds` stamps one of the listed vaults on every floor of the band.

A draft only shapes the grid. `LayoutStrategy` (`layoutStrategy.ts`) owns the guarantees every layout keeps: the forced vault (`forcedVaultId`) and 1–2 random eligible vaults stamp through `VaultStamper`, with connectors carved out to the network and vault interiors never carved; every walkable cell outside a vault joins the network (secret caches through their secret door, each cache's cells handed on as `GeneratedFloorData.secretCaches` and stocked with a chest by `stockSecretCaches` from a PRNG of its own, so the floor's other draws are as they were); the down stairs are the farthest floor tile reachable without secrets; the spawn is never in a vault or next to a hazard; and `rooms` is `RectRoom[]` with the spawn room first (built rooms, or virtual rooms cut from organic floor on a coarse grid). A draft that can't meet these after eight attempts falls back to rooms and corridors. Every choice draws from the floor's seed. `layoutStrategies.test.ts` checks all of this across seeds for each strategy, including the floor-5, -22 and -45 forced vaults.

A band may set a `threshold` (`ThresholdRoomDefinition`: rows of layout characters with one `@`), the room its first floor opens in so a new zone arrives as an event. After the draft, `LayoutStrategy` carves it near the draft's own spawn: its footprint covers only rock and plain floor (no liquid, chasm, vault interior or secret within a cell), keeps two cells from every vault, and never puts the arrival where the draft wants the down stairs. Sites are ranked by how little open floor they cover and how close they are to the draft's spawn. Each walkable edge cell is an exit tunnelled out to the network, and later tunnels go around the room, not through it. The player arrives on the `@` (the up stairs); the room is `rooms[0]` and every other room is clipped clear of it, so nothing is populated there. A draft whose threshold finds no site counts as a failed attempt. Rooms and corridors ignores `threshold`.

`cotw` uses one strategy per zone (`src/content/cotw/floorLayouts.ts`) on 57x40 floors: `caverns` with a black-ice mere (1–9; the band splits at 6 so the Draugr Barrow landmark, guarded by a floor-14 Ancient Draugr, stands on floors 6–9 only), `halls` with the forge pit (10–17), `rift` with the Siphon Pylon landmark (18–25), `lattice` with a sump (26–33), `warrens` with a knothole grove (34–42) and `spine` with the eye pool (43–49). Its forced vaults (floor 5 rune chamber, floor 22 Siphon Altar, floor 45 fang vault) stamp through each strategy; `zoneLayouts.test.ts` checks them per zone. Each zone's first floor opens in its threshold room (`thresholds.ts`): an ice grotto at the foot of the cellar stair (1), the dwarven gatehouse (10), a ledge over the rift with one natural bridge (18), the mine head between two old shafts (26), a hollow in a knot of the World Tree (34) and the jaws of the Maw (43). The Rotting Root has none; its only floor is the Heartwood lair.

The boss floor is `DungeonArc.generateBossLair`. When the quest's `bossFloorLayout.layout` is set (rows of tile characters, with an optional `legend` for pack tiles), the lair is drawn from it, with the boss at `bossSpawn` and its hoard two rows above. The guards are `bossFloorLayout.guards`, each the pack's monster scaled for the floor like any other (a guard whose spot is rock, chasm or taken is left out); a quest that declares none gets the engine's four placeholder bodyguards, arranged around `bossSpawn`, in either hall. `cotw`'s lair is the Heartwood (`lair.ts`).

The town (floor 0) is `TownMapGenerator`. By default it is an open courtyard with each of `town.buildings` walled in. When `town.layout` is set (rows: `#` rock or wall, `.` ground, `+`/`'` doors, `>` stairs down, and a `legend` from other characters to the pack's tile types), the town is drawn from the rows and takes their size; `buildings` then only name the interiors (merchants, and the renderer's `town_<buildingType>` look). Legend tiles resolve from the pack's `tiles` before an engine exists (a new character starting in town) and from the tile registry after. `town.lit` puts the town in daylight: sight there reaches as far as line of sight goes instead of the hero's radius, and the renderer draws no torch falloff or warm pool there (emissive tiles still glow); cotw sets it.

`cotw`'s Bjarnarhaven (`townLayout.ts`) is a 56x36 clearing in the pines, built from shapes and a fixed seed: cobbled lanes wind from the main street to every door and the cellar stairs, a plaza holds a frozen fountain, and statues, carts, market stalls, barrels and woodpiles are the pack's own impassable tiles. Any open cell the lanes don't reach becomes woods. `townLayout.test.ts` checks that every NPC, door and the stairs are reachable and each shopkeeper stands inside their building.


## Levels
`manifest.progressionConfig` (`ProgressionConfig`, `types/config.ts`) is a pack's level shape: `maxLevel`, the XP curve (`getXpForNextLevel`, or `baseXp` with `xpExponent`), `statGains`, `statPointsPerLevel`, `levelUpHeal` (the share of each bar a level restores: a base plus a step per Constitution point above a baseline, capped; absent, a level restores both in full) and `respawnXpShare`, what a monster refilling a visited floor is worth (cleared-floor respawns and catch-up spawns in `FloorManager`; a floor's first population and wandering spawns pay in full). `Player.gainXp` reports what the level healed, and `awardPlayerXp` logs only the gains the pack's level gives. Without one the engine's own curve applies (75·L − 25 a level, uncapped). `Player.isAtLevelCap` tells the HUD and Character tab to show the bar full and the XP "at its height"; `totalXp` keeps counting past the cap, so the score does. `cotw` (`content/cotw/progression.ts`, Q4 "B", Q25, tracker 3.1) caps at 50 and asks `90 · level^1.5` a level: a full clear's 554k XP, the same on every difficulty, reaches level 47 at about a level a floor (6 at floor 5, 9 at 10, 22 at 25, 40 at 45); 48–50 come from revisiting (respawns pay a quarter) or the Mead of Suttungr, a `gain_level` consumable (`ConsumableEffectDescriptor`: it pays the remaining XP of each level through `awardPlayerXp`, so the level-up logs and emits like a kill's, and `gain_xp` now goes the same way; a potion that only grants levels is refused and kept at the cap), about one a game on floors 20–24. `npm run balance` prints the level per floor; `progression.test.ts` pins the full clear at 46–48.

## Attributes in Combat
`manifest.combatConfig.attributeScaling` (`AttributeScalingConfig`, `types/config.ts`; tracker 3.2) says what the four attributes do, per point above a `baseline` (10): `meleeDamagePerStrength` is added to a melee blow before the foe's defense comes off; `meleeBaseHitPercent` and `meleeHitPercentPerDexterity` give melee a hit roll (100 and 0, the defaults, mean every blow lands as before), rolled before the defender's evasion; `evasionPerDexterity` joins the evasion the defender wears and the Survivor perk, never below 0; `spellPowerPerIntelligence` multiplies a spell's damage (chain lightning included) and healing. Each is read in one place through `combat/attributeScaling.ts` (`strengthMeleeBonus`, `meleeHitPercent`, `dexterityEvasion`, `spellPowerMultiplier`), and an actor without the attribute, a monster say, counts as the baseline. A pack that declares none keeps attack − defense melee that always lands. The Character tab's previews, "If you accept" rows and the belt's and Spellbook's power numbers read the same formulas (`spellPower(spell, { engine, player })`). `cotw` (`content/cotw/combat.ts`, Q5 "C", Q28): +1 melee damage a Strength point, 80% + 2% a Dexterity point to hit and 1% evasion, +3% spell power an Intelligence point; a level gives one point, +5 HP and +4 Seiðr, no Attack or Defense, and heals 30% + 3% a Constitution point above 10 of each bar, up to 90% (`progression.ts`). Measured by `.prompts/phase3/attr-duels.ts`.

## Perks
`manifest.perks` (`PerkDefinition`, `types/perks.ts`; tracker 3.6, Q27 "Separate sources.") declares the lasting rules a hero can earn, each with a `source` (saga, milestone, family) and `effects` in the item-modifier vocabulary (`PerkEffects`: every `ItemModifier` field but an item's identity), so each effect is read where the matching worn effect is read: `wornModifiers` returns the hero's `Player.perkModifiers` beside what it wears, and `sumWorn`/`lowestWorn`/`wearsFlag` need no change. An effect a perk needs that no item has is added to `ItemModifier`, read in one place, and is then an item effect too. A perk is granted by a choice's `grantPerk` consequence (`Player.grantPerk`; one already held is left alone) and saved by id (`SerializedPlayer.perks`), resolved against the pack on load, so no migration is needed; a `modifyAttribute` consequence raises an attribute for good outside the point pool (`Player.modifyAttribute`). `manifest.levelMilestones` (`LevelMilestoneTrigger`) offers a choice once the hero reaches a level, the same one-time `<id>_offered` idiom as attribute milestones, checked each move in `movement.ts` and held through a prologue. The Character tab lists the Saga levels among the milestones and the perks held in a card of their own. `cotw` (`content/cotw/perks.ts`, Q51–Q53 approved 2026-10-04) offers three Saga perks at each of levels 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 (Berserkergang, Seiðr-Woven, Wayfarer; Shield-Wall, Blood-Drinker, Spell-Thief; Twin Fangs, Elementalist, Beast-Friend; Einherjar, Galdr-Master, Shadow-Walker; Jarl of the Deep, Odin's Eye, Thor's Wrath), Elementalist choosing its element (fire, cold, lightning, poison or arcane) in a follow-up choice; and two milestone perks at each attribute's 20, 25 and 30 (20: Ox-Shoulders / Bone-Breaker, Fleet-Foot / Sure Shot, Thick Hide / Iron Stomach, Rune-Thrift / Lore-Keeper, the old +2/+3 Attack or Defense pairs gone; 25: Sunder / Giant's Grip, Riposte / Trap-Dancer, Second Wind / Stalwart, Chain-Weaver / Warding Glyph; 30: Mountain's Root / Hammer of Thor, Shadow-Step / Deadly Precision, Undying / Juggernaut, Arch-Seiðkona / Mind over Matter); and one family perk for each of the eight monster families (Q53 "A": Grave-Warden, Pack-Sense, Reaver, Giant-Bane, Spirit-Ward, Rust-Touch, Iron Will, Wyrm-Bane). A family perk is a `PerkDefinition` with `source: 'family'` and the family's `category`: it is offered beside the shared five when that family is mastered (`masteryPerkOptions`), chosen and saved in the compendium like them (one per family, changed only in town), never granted to the hero, and its effects count only against that family's members (`familyModifiers`, read through `sumAgainst`/`productAgainst` at the sites that know the foe: melee in both directions, wind-ups, spell damage and afflictions, kills' XP and coin, sensing). A `modifyPermanentStat` consequence may also set `speed` (Fleet-Foot).

## Coinage
`manifest.coinage` (`CoinageDefinition`, `types/manifest.ts`) sets a pack's coin scale by floor: `pileValueCp(floor)`, the mean worth of one ordinary pile in copper (a pile is worth half to one and a half times it), `metalWeights(floor)`, how likely copper, silver and gold are, and `maxPileCoins`. `mintCoinPile` (`economy/currency.ts`) mints a pile from it, stepping down a metal rather than mint one or two dear coins and up one rather than mint a heap of cheap ones. Ground piles, chest coins (rooms, vaults, monster chest drops) and the boss hoard (ten piles) use it when the pack sets it; otherwise `spawnFloorCurrency` keeps its built-in table (`warcraft`). `cotw`'s curve (`content/cotw/coinage.ts`, Q43 2026-10-04) puts income on the shop's copper scale: about 400 CP for a full clear of floor 1, 1,500 on floor 10, 6,000 on floor 49, about 150k a game, copper and silver piles early and mostly gold deep. Its monsters mint their purses from the same curve through `coinDrop(richness)`, where richness is ordinary piles of the death floor (1 for an ordinary monster; more for strong ones; 5–8 for minibosses, wardens and Níðhögg). `npm run balance` measures the result per floor.

## Loot volume
`manifest.loot` (`LootRatesDefinition`, `types/manifest.ts`) sets how much a floor holds: per room past the arrival room, the chance of one loose drop and the share of those that are coin piles (`roomDropChance`, `roomCoinShare`), the chance of a chest (`roomChestChance`); how many entries a chest holds and the share that are coins (`chestEntries`, `chestCoinShare`; room, vault and monster chests and the boss hoard); how many a secret cache's chest holds (`cacheEntries`, default 2–3, cotw 1–2); and the newest-item rule of `selectFloorItemDefinition`: `newestShare` of draws come from the `newestDefinitions` newest definitions the floor has unlocked (by `minFloor`, definitions sharing one kept together), the rest from the older ones. Within a group a definition is drawn by its `ItemDefinition.lootWeight` (default 1). A field left out keeps the built-in rate (60% drop, 40% coins, 25% chest, 2–4 entries, 35% coins, 75% over the definitions of the newest floor alone), which is what `warcraft` gets. Monster drops are the pack's loot tables. Where a drop or chest lies is `pickLootTile` (`dungeon/itemPlacement.ts`, tracker 5.7, N22): open floor in the room's box, never a door, stairs, water, a trap, a fixture, a 1-wide passage, other loot or a secret cache's cell; a chest against a wall, in a corner or alcove where the room has one. Room loot left under a fixture stamped afterwards moves aside, and a dead monster's drop lands off a door, stairs or water (`dropTile`). Every roll now lands; before, one on a wall placed nothing. `cotw` (`content/cotw/loot.ts`, Q2 "C", tracker 2.4) aims at about 8–10 items a floor (it was 17–29): 36% / 55%, one room in eighteen a chest of 1–3 entries (55% and one in twelve before 5.7, when about a third of rolls were lost), 75% over the eight newest definitions; its monster tables state each item drop's weight through `itemDrop(weight, itemId)`, scaled by `COTW_MONSTER_DROP_SCALE` (gear 0.4, consumables 0.5), and its four cursed relics draw at `lootWeight` 0.25. Measured over a full clear: about 8.7 items a floor (9.75 with the secret caches stocked and 5.7's placement), no definition above a sixth of a floor's draws, and with `itemsPerGame` re-measured, 60 Positive, 45 Negative (relics included) and 10 Chaotic a game. `lootVolume.test.ts` pins those.

## Traps
`manifest.trapPlacement` (`TrapPlacementConfig`) sets how many of the pack's `traps` a generated floor hides, by bands like `floorEncounters`: the entry with the greatest `minFloor` not deeper than the floor gives a count range, drawn evenly. `placeFloorTraps` (`dungeon/trapPlacement.ts`) runs last in `DungeonArc.generateProceduralFloor`, on a stream of its own (`seed + floor × 1299709`), so a floor's monsters, items and caches are those it would have without traps. Each trap is a definition the floor allows (`TrapDefinition.minFloor`/`maxFloor`, drawn by `weight`; `concealment` and `disarmDifficulty` copied) on a site the hero reaches without a secret door: plain `floor`, no item or creature, outside vaults and the threshold room, at least 6 from the arrival and 3 from the down stairs, never beside another trap; passages (cells in no room) fill before rooms. Ids are `trap-<floor>-<n>`. The town, the prologue and the boss lair are not generated here and hide none; a pack without `trapPlacement` hides none. cotw (owner, 2026-10-07): pits from floor 1, darts from 3, alarms from 5, teleport runes from 8; 0–1 a floor on 1–4, 1–2 on 5–9, 1–3 on 10–24, 2–4 from 25. A trap springs under whoever steps on it, hero or monster, hidden or revealed, until it is disarmed (a sprung pit or dart fires again on the next step), and ends the step if it kills or moves the mover; it is found by passive perception or Search (`movement.ts`, `search.ts`). A revealed trap that isn't disarmed is a wall to monsters, as it is to the hero's click-travel (`findAStarPath`): `isRevealedTrap` (`ai/pathfinding.ts`) bars it from every way a monster picks a step (`findPath`, the distance field, flee, search and flank steps), so a monster waits behind a revealed pit that fills a corridor rather than walking in (owner, 2026-10-07). A hidden trap still springs under a monster, and a monster killed by one pays the hero XP like any killer-less death. A trap a monster springs where the hero can't see it logs nothing and stays hidden (`CombatLogger.isVisibleToPlayer`): not revealed, its tile unchanged, so neither the log nor the map gives away a trap or a monster out of sight, and monsters don't yet route round it. A teleport rune lands on a free cell reachable from it without a secret door, never in a cache or a sealed vault cell. `trapPlacement.test.ts` sweeps floors 1–49.
