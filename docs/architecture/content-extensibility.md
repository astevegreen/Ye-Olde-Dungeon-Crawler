# Content Extensibility Model

> How a content pack plugs into the engine, and why; [`ARCHITECTURE.md`](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) §3 holds the rules and wins any disagreement (flag it, §8.2). Values a pack sets (cotw's HP, prices, perks, zones) are in its code, not here. Companions have their own doc: [content-companions.md](content-companions.md).

## Declarative Manifests
Each content pack exports a `GameContentManifest` (`src/engine/types/manifest.ts`), whose entries the `GameEngine` constructor registers: `monsters`, `spells`, `traps`, `statusEffects`/`statusHandlers`, `aiBehaviors`/`aiStrategies`, `actionHooks`, `affinityMatrix`, `pacts`, `progressionConfig`, `initialWorldState`, `quest`, `theme`, and the optional systems below. Content assembles behavior from composable primitives: status afflictions, spawners, stat triggers, damage affinities, drop tables and quest arcs. A status handler marked `affliction` (poison, paralysis, slow, blindness, stun) is one a cure of every affliction (`cure_status: 'all'`) purges; an unmarked status (haste, a channel, a floor's darkness) stays.

An optional section a pack leaves out either turns its system off or falls back to a neutral built-in default; each section below says which. Presentation wording rides along: `branding` and `town.name` word the shared screens (the experience and mana terms included), and `firstTimeHints` gives the note the HUD shows once per hero when a system first appears.

## Two Hook Mechanisms
1. **Action hooks** (`manifest.actionHooks`, interface `ActionHook` in `actionPipeline.ts`): priority-ordered `pre`/`post` hooks around pipeline-executed actions. They receive `ActionHookContext` (`action`, `engine`, `actionType`, and `result` in the post phase). A pre-hook can short-circuit with its own `ActionResult`. Hooks fire for every actor; content that should react only to the player compares `ActionHookContext.actor` against `engine.player`.
2. **Declarative event hooks** (`HookDispatcher`, `src/engine/hooks/hookDispatcher.ts`): `HookDescriptor` data (event, chance, predicate, primitive action) on items and monsters. They receive `HookContext` (`engine`, `attacker`, `defender`, `damage`, …). The action is one of the engine's built-in primitives (`ActionPrimitive`: `applyStatus`, `spawnSurface`, `spawnGas`, `heal`, `bonusDamage`); content cannot register another, and there are no global hooks. Every way a monster is made from its definition (floor, vault, wandering and boss spawns, and a load) carries the definition's `hooks`.
   - `onTurnStart` fires at the start of an awake monster's turn, after its status tick, unless it is stunned or paralyzed; its `defender` is the entity the monster engages when that one is within 8 tiles and in line of sight. A primitive aimed at `target: 'target'` with no one there is skipped, never turned on the hook's owner.
   - `onHit` (the attacker's hooks) and `onDamageTaken` (the defender's, when wounded) fire for every blow with an attacker: melee, spell damage, chain-lightning hops and monster wind-ups (`dispatchDamageHooks`, `src/engine/hooks/damageHooks.ts`), before the blow's death resolves; not for a blow the defender was immune to or absorbed. `onBlock` is melee's alone.

## Injected Context
Handlers get the live engine through `EngineContext` (`src/engine/types/engineContext.ts`), never the raw `GameEngine`: `player`, `map`, `surfaces`, `worldState`, the seeded `rng`, `log()`, the world-flag accessors, `currentFloor`, `removeEntity()`/`addEntity()` (through the scheduler, unlike `map.removeEntity`), and `compendium` narrowed to `getEntry`. `GameEngine` satisfies it structurally, so nothing is wrapped at runtime and the type checker enforces the narrowing: a handler reaching for `engine.scheduler` doesn't compile. Widen it deliberately: each member added joins the content-facing contract. The dispatcher's own built-in primitives (damage, death, statuses, surfaces) are engine code, not handlers, and narrow back through one documented cast.

## Content Registries
Manifest registration writes into per-engine registries owned by `GameEngine` (`engine.registries`, `EngineRegistries`). Content registries (monsters, traps, spells, companions, AI strategies, AI behaviors, status handlers, tiles) are seeded from process defaults when the engine is created and supplemented by the manifest, so two engines built from different manifests do not collide. Runtime registries (`containers`, `itemIndex`) are per-engine and filled from the live world, not from defaults. Module-level facades (`MonsterRegistry`, `SpellRegistry`, `CompanionRegistry`, `StatusHandlerRegistry`, the tile lookups `getTileDefinition`/`hasTileDefinition`, `containerRegistry`, `itemIndex`) forward into whichever engine's store is active (`activateRegistries(engine.registries)`) and hold no state of their own. Traps have no facade: an engine's trap definitions are `engine.registries.traps`.

## Pack-Neutral Presentation
The rule is ARCHITECTURE.md §3: `src/ui/`, `src/rendering/` and `src/main/**` name no pack. This is where each kind of pack-specific presentation comes from.
- **Wording:** a pack's title, tagline, town and flavor come from the manifest (`name`, `description`, `town.name`, `branding`).
- **Sprites and icons:** art comes from `spriteRecipes`. A recipe keyed by a monster or item definition ID, or by an NPC's id, is that entity's sprite (its own `SpriteAtlas` cell); the pack's own creature tags map to its recipes through `atlas.spriteTagRules`, checked before the renderer's generic `DEFAULT_TAG_SPRITE_ORDER`. One keyed `ui~<name>` is that UI icon: the vocabulary is `src/ui/icons.ts`, DOM shows it as `ui-icon` elements baked at startup by `installUiIcons`, canvas overlays draw it through `canvasIcons.ts`, and an icon the pack doesn't draw doesn't show. One keyed `spell~<spellId>`, else `spell~<elementId>`, is a spell's rune (`src/ui/spellRunes.ts`); a spell's color is its element's in `affinityMatrix`, else its own `visual.color`. Presentation, F2 diagnostics included, shows these icons, not emoji.
- **Self-lit sprites and idle frames:** a `pixelSprites` entry (`PixelSprite`), keyed like a recipe and drawn before one of the same key, hands the atlas finished RGBA pixels: the pack lights, outlines and shadows it, so the atlas's shading and outline passes and the renderer's generic entity shadow skip it. It may have up to four idle frames, baked one at a time on first use; the map cycles them on an ambient tick (`src/rendering/atlas/idle-frames.ts`) that never holds input (ARCHITECTURE.md §4), holds frame 0 in remembered views, and holds still under the player's Reduce motion setting, which follows the system's reduced-motion preference until the player picks. `heroSprite` (`HeroSpriteArt`) draws the hero in their gear: the pack names a look from what is equipped, and the atlas keeps the last twelve looks. Without it the hero is the `player`/`player_female` sprite.
- **Item auras:** `itemAuras` (`ItemAuraArt`) draws an identified item's family (cursed, hexed, unholy, chaotic, enchanted, blessed, holy, artifact; the same one its name and icon frame show) as an aura around and over its sprite: the pack takes the item's finished pixels and returns them with the aura, frame by frame, up to sixteen. The atlas bakes each sprite, family and frame once (`src/rendering/atlas/item-auras.ts`); an item that idles keeps its own frames inside the loop. The map's ground items and every menu icon show it on the ambient tick, menu icons repainting while they are on the page, and all hold frame 0 under Reduce motion. An unidentified item shows plain.
- **Spell and melee art:** `spellFx` (`SpellFxCatalog`) draws effects as pack art instead of the renderer's generic orbs, rings and arcs. Its `elements` are keyed by an effect's `fx`, which the spell pipeline sets to the spell's `visual.fx` or else its element, so a pack maps engine elements to art by listing both names (cotw: `cold` and `frost` share the frost art) and gives a school its own look by `visual.fx` (blood magic). Each entry draws a `bolt`, an `impact` and optionally a `self` cast onto `FxSurface`, a structural slice of the 2D context, so content stays DOM-free (§2); the runner (`src/rendering/fxRunner.ts`) translates it to world pixels so tile-seeded art holds still while the camera scrolls. Pacing stays the engine's: a bolt's life is stretched over the flight time, an impact holds input for its first 250 ms and plays the rest as ambient, a burst or self cast holds as long as its generic shape did, and a flight that lands on a burst's epicentre leaves the impact to the burst. The targeting overlay's after-image of the flight is drawn only for spells without art. `melee` draws a hit, critical or miss on the target of every visible `damage_dealt` and `attack_missed` event, ambient. An effect with no entry, and a pack without `spellFx` (the fixture pack), draws the generic shapes.
- **Terrain:** a pack that declares `atlas.terrain` gets neighbour-aware terrain (`src/rendering/atlas/terrain-layers.ts`: floor tone variants by world-space noise, wall faces and rims, liquids and pits blended as one area, props drawn over the floor), its `memory` look for remembered cells and its `torch`/`emissive` light. Without it a pack keeps one recipe per tile type (`getTerrainSpriteKey`), and `drawTacticalTerrain` marks water, chasms, bars and pillars over it in role colors. `src/rendering/` names no zone or floor band.
- **Surfaces and gases:** `atlas.overlays` maps `surface~<type>` and `gas~<type>` to a `CellOverlayArt`, which is not baked into the atlas but runs every frame over the terrain, given the cell and the frame's clock, so fire can flicker without content reading the clock. A type the pack draws no art for shows as a neutral wash in role colors (`IntentOverlay.renderSurfaces`), so a hazard never acts unseen.
- **Theme tokens:** color, type, radius and fonts come from the semantic role tokens of the pack's `ThemeTokens`. `resolveThemeTokens`/`applyThemeTokens` in `src/rendering/theme.ts` write them as `--ui-*` variables; the static type, space, layer and motion scales are in `src/ui/styles/tokens.css` ([ADR-0011](../decisions/0011-menus-and-visual-system-direction.md)). Presentation names roles (`var(--ui-accent)`, `theme.textMuted`), never hues.
- **Text size and UI scale:** canvas UI text is sized with `uiFont()`, which holds the 11px floor at every window size (map glyphs and cell badges scale with the cell instead). The player's UI scale (`src/ui/uiScale.ts`, Auto by default) zooms the interface's DOM roots (never the map canvas, which fits to what they leave) and `uiFont()`'s sizes alike.

## Items and Item Families
Loot may belong to one of seven families: Blessed, Enchanted, Holy (positive), Cursed, Hexed, Unholy (negative) and Chaotic, separate from its +N roll ([ADR-0012](../decisions/0012-item-families-as-pack-data.md)). The engine owns the family ids, the three alignments and what each modifier field does; `manifest.itemFamilies` (`ItemFamilyConfig`) supplies the tiers (names, numbers, the floor each starts on), each family's expected count per game, whether it binds, and `itemsPerGame`. `rollItemFamily` (`src/engine/items/modifierRoller.ts`) rolls Normal first, then a family by `perGame / itemsPerGame`, then the deepest tier the floor has reached. Only loot rolls a family (`createScaledItem` given the config: floor loot, chests, the boss hoard, monster drops); shop stock, starting gear and rewards stay Normal. `ItemDefinition.family` pins a definition to one family. Without `itemFamilies` every item is Normal.

What a modifier does is its fields (`ItemModifier`), each read in one place, so an effect means the same whether a family, a definition's `wornEffects` or a perk carries it: `wornModifiers` returns what the hero wears beside the perks it holds. Damage taken and healing received scale in `Actor.takeDamage`/`Actor.heal`, so they apply whatever dealt the blow or the cure. A family perk's effects (`familyModifiers`) count only against that monster family's members, read through `sumAgainst`/`productAgainst` where the foe is known.

Tag-filtered radial auras: a consumable's `radial_status` effect applies a status to every entity within a radius that has any of its `tags` (`findTaggedEntitiesInRadius`, `src/engine/combat/radialAuraFilter.ts`, over `MonsterDefinition.tags` and `Entity.hasTag()`). The query is bounded (§6) and the match a flat any-of list, not a boolean expression tree, as `hasTag()` is used everywhere else.

## Magic
`manifest.magic` (`MagicSystemConfig`, `src/engine/magic/magicConfig.ts`) turns on four optional systems. The engine owns each mechanism and only neutral defaults; every table, label and message is the pack's.

| Section | Mechanism | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| `overflow` | `magic/manaOverflow.ts`: a cast short of mana goes off; the shortfall is debt (`Player.voidDebt`), and each such cast rolls a weighted surge from the tier the total debt has reached. Rest pays debt down. | A hard mana wall (`canOvercast`). |
| `grimoire` | `magic/grimoireMatrix.ts`: a 3x3 grid per page, three pages, one copy of a spell per page. A player cast resolves through its slot: center-slot trade, element synergies, the slot's ground element, inscribed glyphs (`SpellModifier` data). Page switches and slot edits are actions (`AttuneGrimoirePageAction`, `ArrangeGrimoireSlotAction`), so the replay trail records them. | Spells cast unmodified; no grid. |
| `killRites` | `magic/killRites.ts`: `MonsterDefinition.killRite` names conditions on a death the player controls (killing-blow element, victim status, overkill, debt, HP). Meeting them teaches a spell once, else drops the element's essence. `DeathResolver.resolveDeath` takes a `KillContext`. | No rites. |
| `altars`/`hybrids` | `magic/altars.ts`: an altar tile emits `altar_reached`; the rite runs as `PerformAltarRiteAction` (replayable): inscribe a glyph, forge a hybrid spell, open a sealed slot, or gamble. Each altar position is single-use. | No altars. |

Generic effect primitives (`spellPipeline.ts`) give spells, scrolls and tablets powers no pack has to code: `learn_spell` teaches one, `uncurse` breaks the curses on everything the caster wears and carries (`breakCurses`), and `summon` places a monster beside the caster, which with a `duration` carries the built-in `summoned` status (saved) and leaves when it expires.

## Progression
**Levels and attributes.** `manifest.progressionConfig` (`ProgressionConfig`, `types/config.ts`) is the level shape: `maxLevel`, the XP curve, `statGains`, `statPointsPerLevel`, `levelUpHeal`, and `respawnXpShare` (what a monster refilling a visited floor pays). Without it the engine's curve applies (75·L − 25 a level, uncapped). `manifest.combatConfig.attributeScaling` (`AttributeScalingConfig`) says what each attribute point above a baseline adds to melee damage, hit chance, evasion and spell power, each read in one place (`combat/attributeScaling.ts`); the Character tab's previews and the belt's numbers read the same formulas. Without it melee is attack − defense and always lands.

**Perks.** `manifest.perks` (`PerkDefinition`, `types/perks.ts`) are lasting rules a hero earns, each with a `source` (saga, milestone, family) and `effects` in the item-modifier vocabulary (`PerkEffects`), so a perk's effect is read wherever the matching worn effect is. An effect a perk needs that no item has becomes an `ItemModifier` field, read in one place, and items can carry it too. A choice's `grantPerk` grants one; perks are saved by id and resolved against the pack on load, so a new perk needs no migration. A family perk (`source: 'family'`, with the family's `category`) is offered when the hero masters that family (`masteryPerkOptions`), saved in the compendium, and counts only against that family.

**Milestone Renown Ledger.** `manifest.renownMilestones` and `renownTitles` declare per-category renown and titles. The engine calls `recordMilestone(engine, id)` (`src/engine/renown/renownLedger.ts`) at generic trigger points (a curse broken, a secret door found), a no-op when no milestone has that id: the engine offers the hook, content may react. A choice records one with the `recordMilestone` consequence; content code awards its own with `awardMilestone(ctx, def)` (a bare `modifyCounter` skips the HUD's total). Renown rides on `WorldState` counters and flags (`renown:<category>`, `renown_milestone_earned:<id>`), so it needed no save field, and a vendor unlock is a `Predicate.minCounter` on such a counter.

**Monster power.** `manifest.monsterScaling` (`MonsterScalingConfig`, `types/monsterScaling.ts`) is a step function over `{floor, multiplier}` tiers (the highest at or below the floor applies, not interpolated), shifted by a difficulty's `basePowerMultiplier` and `scalingRateMultiplier` (growth above 1.0 only), with a `bossPowerFloorMultiplier` so a low difficulty can't trivialize a boss or miniboss. HP and attack take the whole multiplier, defense the multiplier to `defenseExponent`: a hero's melee grows by flat weapon steps, which a full-multiplier defense outran. XP follows the zone tier alone, so difficulty raises the fight, not its reward. Without the config the original flat curve applies unchanged.

Every random monster draw (floor population, vault fill, wandering spawns) goes through `selectDungeonMonsterDefinition` (`dungeon/spawner.ts`): monsters not `placedOnly` whose `minFloor` the floor has reached, weighted by `dungeonSpawnWeight` and falling by the cube of their age (half six floors on), so a floor's mix is led by its own stretch of the dungeon. It replaced a draw that flooded floors where one monster unlocked alone. A vault's `preferredMonsters` hold their spots only while the drawn guard's weight is at least 0.1.

**Economy.** `manifest.coinage` (`CoinageDefinition`) sets the coin scale by floor (`mintCoinPile`, `economy/currency.ts`), and `manifest.loot` (`LootRatesDefinition`) the drop, coin-share and chest rates and the newest-item rule (`selectFloorItemDefinition`, weighted by `ItemDefinition.lootWeight`); a field left out keeps the built-in rate. Where loot lies is `pickLootTile` (`dungeon/itemPlacement.ts`): open floor in the room, never a door, stairs, water, trap, fixture, 1-wide passage or cache cell, and a chest against a wall. Town services are pack data too: `manifest.merchantPricing` (prices by faction standing), each merchant's `markupRatio`/`markdownRatio`, and `town.services` (temple gates, offerings and blessings, `smiths`, `monsterLore`); a pack that declares none gets flat prices and no such service. `npm run balance` measures levels, income and loot per floor.

## Floors
`manifest.floorLayouts` (`FloorLayoutBand[]`) picks a layout strategy per floor band, tuned by `params` (each documented on its draft in `drafts.ts`); a quest's `floorGenerators[floor]` still wins, and other floors use the quest's `defaultGenerator` (else `bsp`). `manifest.floorSize` sets the size (default 50x35). Besides `bsp` and `cavern`, `DungeonGeneratorRegistry` holds six character-grid strategies (`src/engine/dungeon/layout/`).

A draft only shapes the grid. `LayoutStrategy` (`layoutStrategy.ts`) owns what every layout keeps: the forced vault and 1–2 random eligible vaults stamp through `VaultStamper`, joined to the network, their interiors never carved; every walkable cell outside a vault joins the network (secret caches through their secret door, stocked by `stockSecretCaches` from a PRNG of their own, so the floor's other draws don't move); the down stairs are the farthest tile reachable without secrets; the spawn is never in a vault or beside a hazard; `rooms` lists the spawn room first. A draft that misses these eight times falls back to rooms and corridors. Every choice draws from the floor's seed; `layoutStrategies.test.ts` checks it all across seeds. A band's `threshold` (`ThresholdRoomDefinition`, rows with one `@`) is the room its first floor opens in, so a new zone arrives as an event: carved near the draft's spawn over rock and plain floor only, clear of vaults and the down stairs, each edge exit tunnelled to the network.

`manifest.roomDecoration` and `manifest.floorHazards` tune decoration and the run advisor's elemental warning per floor band; with none the engine uses neutral defaults and warns of nothing. Both replaced ranges once hard-coded in the engine.

**Traps.** `manifest.trapPlacement` (`TrapPlacementConfig`) sets a count range per floor band. `placeFloorTraps` (`dungeon/trapPlacement.ts`) runs last, on a stream of its own (`seed + floor × 1299709`), so a floor's monsters, items and caches are those it would have without traps. Sites are reachable without a secret door, outside vaults and the threshold room, away from the arrival and the down stairs, never beside another trap. A trap springs under whoever steps on it; a revealed one is a wall to every monster path (`isRevealedTrap`, `ai/pathfinding.ts`) as to click-travel; one sprung out of the hero's sight logs nothing and stays hidden, so neither log nor map gives away what the hero can't see. A pack without `trapPlacement` hides none.

The boss floor is `DungeonArc.generateBossLair`, drawn from `quest.bossFloorLayout` (rows plus a `legend`) when set, its `guards` scaled like any monster. The town (floor 0) is `TownMapGenerator`: an open courtyard with each of `town.buildings` walled in, or `town.layout`'s rows when set; `town.lit` lights it to the line of sight (§6).

## Quests, Triggers and Endings
**Choices.** `manifest.choices` holds every decision. A tile whose `interactionHandlerId` names one opens it on step (`actions/movement.ts`) and resolves at most once (`<handlerId>_resolved`, set only when an option is chosen, so cancelling leaves it re-triggerable); an option marked `keepsOpen` doesn't resolve it, and `resolvedStates` gives the tile its lasting message. An NPC with a `choiceId` opens its choice instead of a greeting, at no turn cost, and never resolves; its options gate themselves with predicates. Every trigger applies an option's consequences through one interpreter, `applyConsequences` (exported beside `ExecuteChoiceAction`), so a consequence (`modifyPermanentStat` on the raw base stat, `grantCompanion`, `learnSpell`, `cureStatus`, `grantPerk`, `recordMilestone`, …) means the same wherever it fires.

**Progress triggers.** `storyChoiceTriggers` open a choice once a monster has been slain `killsRequired` times (the compendium's kill count: cross-floor and already saved), with an optional `when` predicate; `levelMilestones` and `attributeMilestones` open one at a level or an attribute threshold, once each (`<id>_offered`). They are checked every player move in `movement.ts`: not in an action hook, whose `EngineContext` can't reach the compendium or open a modal, and not in `Player.allocateAttribute`, which stays a pure mutator and never opens a choice over the Character tab. They wait while a prologue runs and, in town, until its `aftermathFlag` is set. A hand-placed encounter on one floor uses a scripted vault instead.

**Placements.** `fixedTilePlacements` stamp a tile pattern on a floor, optionally after a choice. `scriptedVaultPlacements` (`{ floor, vaultId, npcs? }`, `dungeonArc.ts`) force a vault onto a floor and fill its `N` markers with NPCs, backed by three generic fields: a vault's `legend` maps extra symbols to the pack's own tiles; `scriptedOnly` keeps a vault out of the random pass; `MonsterDefinition.placedOnly` keeps a monster out of every random draw, the only rule that does (the engine doesn't guess from an id). `DungeonResult.forcedVaultPlaced` reports whether the forced vault stamped. `Entity.setFaction` can make a monster `neutral`: it takes no action, and walking into it trades places.

Worked example: cotw's Siphon Altar (`src/content/cotw/hostageRitual.ts`) composes a scripted placement (vault, altar tile via `legend`, four captive NPCs), a tile choice (the altar), a timed event (the ritual countdown) and two action hooks: a `MovementAction` pre-hook that rescues a captive on bump, and a `'*'` post-hook that starts the countdown and settles the captives. All three routes funnel into one outcome function, and no engine code names any part of it.

**Turn-limited world events.** `manifest.timedEvents` (`TimedEventDefinition`): a `startFlag`/`turnLimit`/`resolvedFlag`/`expireConsequences` countdown ticked once per player turn by an environmental update (`GameEngine.tickTimedEvents`), independent of any modal, so a climactic choice can be time-pressured without a per-campaign timer. The hero cannot rest while one runs (`AutoRestManager.restRefusal`): a rest runs up to a hundred turns at a stroke and would spend the countdown unseen. A countdown must be visible to be fair: its `label` lists it in the sidebar with the turns left.

**Boss resolutions and endings.** A `BossFleeResolution` turns sustained fleeing into a concluded encounter: after `fleeTurnsRequired` turns fleeing on the active floor, `sealedFlag` is set and the monster leaves the floor, optionally leaving a `portalTileId`. Its counterpart for the kill is `QuestArcDefinition.bossSlainFlag`. `QuestArcDefinition.endings` (`EndingDefinition`) are named endings gated by a carried relic, a flag and/or a kill; `GameStateManager.checkVictoryEligible` returns the first eligible one's id, and the flat legacy fields remain the `'default'` ending when `endings` is omitted. `quest.victoryPortalTileId` is stamped where the boss fell and handled by the generic `quest_victory_portal` handler. An ending's `title`, `narrative` and `banner` are shown before the score screen.

**Prologue.** `manifest.prologue` (`PrologueDefinition`, `quest/prologue.ts`) is a short scene on the town map before the run. The engine only stages and undoes it: `beginPrologue` places the hero, monsters and NPCs, and `applyPrologueState` unlights the town and sets the hero's HP floor; the pack's own hooks run it and call `concludePrologue` when it is over. Light and HP floor are not saved, so the deserializer reapplies them. The hero cannot rest while it runs (its clock counts turns). The HP floor is generic (`Entity.setHpFloor`, `wasHeldAtHpFloor`), so a pack can turn a would-be death into a scripted outcome. F2's `engine.diagnostics.endPrologue` ends the engine's half only.

**Story presentation.** The character menu's Story tab reads only manifest data (`atlas.tileZoneBands`, `objectives`, `trackedMilestones` with their riddles, `loreEntries`, `factions`), so its wording is the pack's; each list is a display over existing flags and changes no save format.

## Rune of Return
`src/engine/magic/runeOfReturn.ts`: a limited-charge, two-way recall between dungeon and town. Its rules are the engine's, not a pack's: its pipeline hooks (`createRuneOfReturnActionHooks`) are registered unconditionally beside the built-in status handlers. From the dungeon a channel anchors the departure floor and tile and takes the hero to town; from town it spends a charge and returns to the anchor (refused at no cost with none). The channel is a status effect (`RUNE_OF_RETURN_STATUS`, its scalars in `StatusEffect.data`) that ticks like poison or slow rather than in a bespoke loop; a `pre` hook allows only the actions that sustain it, and a `post` hook compares `player.hp` with its last value after every action, monster turns included, so a wound breaks the channel the instant it lands. An interrupted channel banks part of its turns toward the next try; a charge is spent only on completion. Its three mastery tracks spend the attribute point pool. Once awakened the rune lives in the hero (`player.hasDiscoveredRune`), reached through an innate proxy item (`findRuneOfReturn`).

`manifest.runeOfReturn` supplies the pack's part: `attunementNpcId` (talking to that NPC refills the charges; every message naming it goes through `attunementNpcName`), `trackNames`, `whereaboutsHint`, and `acquisition` (`{ floor, vaultId }`, where the dormant rune lies). Without it there is no in-town refill.
