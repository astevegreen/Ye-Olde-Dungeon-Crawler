## Declarative Manifests
Each content pack exports a `GameContentManifest` (`src/engine/types/manifest.ts`). The `GameEngine` constructor registers its entries, for example: `monsters`, `spells`, `traps`, `statusEffects`/`statusHandlers`, `aiBehaviors`/`aiStrategies`, `actionCommands`, `actionHooks`, `affinityMatrix`, `pacts`, `progressionConfig`, `initialWorldState`, `quest`, and `theme`. Content assembles behavior from composable primitives: status afflictions, spawners, stat triggers, damage affinities, drop tables, and quest arcs. Presentation data rides along: `branding`/`town.name` supply shared screens' pack wording, and a `spriteRecipes` key equal to a definition ID gives that entity its own atlas cell (`SpriteAtlas`). A pack that declares `atlas.terrain` gets neighbour-aware terrain from `src/rendering/atlas/terrain-layers.ts`: floors pick among tone variants by smooth world-space noise (optionally a 2x2 macro tile), walls take a face when open to the south and rim or corner overlays on other open sides, liquids and pits blend as one area with edges toward other tiles, and pillars, bars, stairs, traps, doors and the pack's own tiles draw as props over the floor. Doors between rock east and west sit in the wall line; doors never cast a contact shadow, so a corridor joins its room without a break. Terrain cells bake flat (no sprite outline or diagonal shading), `terrain.memory` replaces the blue-slate dimming of remembered cells, and `terrain.torch`/`emissive` light visible cells (the player's Torchlight setting turns it off). Without `atlas.terrain`, a pack keeps one recipe per tile type (`getTerrainSpriteKey`); `drawTacticalTerrain` no longer picks looks by floor number.

## Two Hook Mechanisms
1. **Action hooks** (`manifest.actionHooks`, interface `ActionHook` in `actionPipeline.ts`): priority-ordered `pre`/`post` hooks around pipeline-executed actions. They receive `ActionHookContext` (`action`, `engine`, `actionType`, and `result` in the post phase). A pre-hook can short-circuit with its own `ActionResult`. Hooks fire for every actor; `ActionHookContext.actor` identifies who is acting, so content that should react only to the player compares it against `engine.player`.
2. **Declarative event hooks** (`HookDispatcher` in `src/engine/hooks/hookDispatcher.ts`): `HookDescriptor` data (event, chance, predicate, primitive action) attached to items and monsters or registered globally. They receive `HookContext` (`engine`, `attacker`, `defender`, `damage`, …). Content can add new primitive types with `HookDispatcher.registerPrimitive`.

## Injected Context
Handlers get the live engine through an injected context object rather than importing engine runtime state. Both context types expose `EngineContext` (`src/engine/types/engineContext.ts`), not the raw `GameEngine`: `player`, `map`, `surfaces`, `worldState`, the seeded `rng`, `log()`, the world-flag accessors, `currentFloor`, and `removeEntity()` (the last two added so a pack's hooks can scope an encounter to its floor and take its NPCs off the map through the scheduler-aware path, not `map.removeEntity`). `GameEngine` satisfies it structurally, so nothing is wrapped at runtime and the narrowing is enforced by the type checker — a handler reaching for `engine.scheduler` no longer compiles. Widen it deliberately: each member added becomes part of the content-facing contract. The dispatcher's own built-in primitives (damage, death, impulse, spell casting) are engine code rather than handlers and narrow back through a single documented cast.

## Content Registries
Manifest registration writes into per-engine registries owned by `GameEngine` via `engine.registries` (`EngineRegistries`). Content registries (monsters, traps, action commands, spells, companions, AI strategies, AI behaviors, status handlers, tiles) are seeded from process defaults upon engine creation and supplemented by manifest registration, so two engines built from different manifests do not collide. Runtime state registries (`containers`, `itemIndex`) are per-engine and isolated, populated dynamically from the live world state rather than seeded from process defaults. Module-level registries (e.g. `MonsterRegistry`, `TrapRegistry`, `ActionRegistry`, `SpellRegistry`, `CompanionRegistry`, `StatusHandlers`, `TileRegistry`, `containerRegistry`, `itemIndex`) act as facades that forward into whichever engine's store is active (`activateRegistries(engine.registries)`), holding no state maps of their own, so each engine maintains independent lookups.
